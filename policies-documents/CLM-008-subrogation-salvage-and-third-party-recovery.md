## 1. Purpose

When Northline pays a loss that someone else caused, or takes title to damaged property that still has value, the Company tries to recover what it reasonably can. This policy governs that effort. Recovery is part of the claim, not an afterthought that begins the day the file closes. It is also not a second claim against the Company's own customer. Subrogation against a third party, salvage of insured property, and contribution from another insurer are different tools. Staff name the tool they are using and follow its rules.

Customers are entitled to know when a recovery will affect them. A deductible may be returned if the recovery is large enough. A release given to a responsible party may destroy Northline's rights if it is signed too broadly. This policy tells adjusters how to protect both the customer's interest and the Company's, and how to stop a pursuit that costs more than it can return.

## 2. Scope

Recovery handles subrogation, contribution between insurers, salvage, and statutory liens the Company is allowed to assert. It does not handle premium collection, ordinary overpayment letters under CLM-005, or restitution for fraud under CLM-007, except that money received is coded correctly whichever unit pursued it. Defense of Northline's insured is not compromised to improve a subrogation case. If those goals conflict, the insured's defense comes first and the conflict is documented by a claim manager.

Independent adjusters note recovery opportunities in the file. They do not send demand letters to third parties in Northline's name unless the assignment letter says they are appointed to do so.

## 3. Definitions

**Subrogation.** The Company's right, after payment, to pursue the person who caused the loss, standing in the insured's place to the extent of the payment. It includes contractual subrogation under the policy and equitable subrogation where the law provides it.

**Made whole.** The rule, in states that apply it, that the insured is entitled to be fully compensated for the loss, including the deductible in the manner the state requires, before the Company keeps a recovery. The state addendum states which rule applies. Adjusters do not assume the Company is always first.

**Salvage.** The remaining value of insured property after a total loss or a replacement, which the policy may transfer to the Company when it pays the loss. Salvage is not subrogation. The buyer of salvage is not the person who caused the loss.

**Contribution.** A claim against another insurer that covers the same loss, based on the other-insurance clauses or on equitable contribution. It is not a claim against the other insurer's policyholder personally, except as that policy responds.

**Waiver.** A written or, in some states, implied surrender of a recovery right. A waiver is given only under Section 8. Casual remarks are not waivers, but they can become evidence in a waiver fight, so casual remarks about "not pursuing this" are not made to adverse parties.

**Recovery threshold.** The minimum expected net recovery that justifies pursuit. The current threshold is $1,000 of expected net recovery after collection cost, unless a smaller pursuit is required to return a customer's deductible and the cost of that limited pursuit is low.

## 4. Governing principles

1. Identify the opportunity while the evidence exists, not after the debris is gone and the witnesses have forgotten.
2. Do not release a responsible party, and do not let the insured release one, until Recovery has seen the release or a manager has approved the wording.
3. The customer's deductible is not forgotten in the distribution. The addendum's made-whole or pro-rata rule is applied in the file with the arithmetic shown.
4. Pursuit stops when the expected net recovery falls below the threshold, when liability is too weak to explain to a court, or when the cost to the insured's defense is too high. Pride is not a reason to continue.
5. Collection conduct is lawful and professional. Recovery staff do not threaten criminal action, do not contact a debtor's employer except as the law allows for a judgment, and do not misstate the amount owed.
6. Salvage buyers are arm's-length. Employees and their households do not buy Northline salvage.
7. Money in is applied to the right claim, coverage part, and deductible. Unapplied recoveries are a finance defect.

## 5. Recognizing the opportunity

The assigned adjuster completes the recovery screen on every paid file before closure, and earlier if evidence will be lost. The screen answers:

- who, other than the insured, may have caused or contributed to the loss;
- what evidence identifies them (contract, police report, product, work order);
- whether the policy preserves subrogation and whether any waiver-of-subrogation endorsement applies;
- whether another insurer has been identified under CLM-002;
- whether damaged property will have salvage value if Northline pays a total loss;
- what evidence must be preserved now.

A "none" answer is acceptable when it is true. A blank screen is not. Fires with a responsible contractor, water losses caused by a third party's work, product failures, auto accidents caused by another driver, and premises losses caused by a tenant's negligence are typical opportunities. Ordinary wear on the insured's own pipe is not a subrogation opportunity against the insured, and it is not an opportunity against the plumber who merely responded to the leak unless the plumber caused the damage.

Waiver-of-subrogation endorsements are read before any demand goes out. If the named insured waived recovery against a landlord or a contractor in the contract the endorsement was meant to support, Recovery closes that target and notes the waiver. Demanding anyway because the other party "can afford it" is a violation of the contract Northline issued.

## 6. Preserving the claim

### 6.1 Evidence

Parts, vehicles, and failed products that may support recovery are held under CLM-003 until Recovery releases them or photographs and tests them. A total-loss vehicle is not sold for salvage the day the customer settles if a subrogation inspection is still needed, unless a manager approves the sale because the liability evidence is already complete. The hold is dated. Property is not stored forever. If no expert inspection is scheduled within thirty days of the hold, Recovery either inspects, releases, or obtains a manager's extension.

### 6.2 Notices and tenders

When another insurer may owe contribution or may insure the responsible party, Recovery or the examiner sends a written tender as soon as the facts support it. The tender includes the claim number, the facts, the amount paid or reserved, and a request for the other carrier's position. Northline continues to meet its own duties while waiting. A tender is not a bill. The amount is updated when the payment is final.

On product and contractor claims, a notice is sent to the potential defendant and, if known, to its insurer, asking that evidence be preserved. The notice is factual. It does not announce a lawsuit that has not been approved.

### 6.3 Releases the insured wants to sign

If an insured is asked to sign a release by a responsible party — a neighbor, a contractor, another driver — before Northline has paid, the adjuster reviews the release or sends it to Recovery the same day. The insured is advised, in writing, not to sign a release that waives Northline's rights until the wording is approved, and is told that signing may affect their claim if the policy requires them to protect those rights. The advice is not legal advice to the third party. If the insured signs despite the warning, the file documents prejudice under CLM-002 only to the extent the recovery was actually lost and the law allows that consequence. The claim is not denied automatically because a release was signed.

A release Northline gives a third party on a liability claim under CLM-005 is checked so it does not accidentally release a subrogation claim against a different party, and so it does not release Northline's own insured from cooperation duties that still matter.

## 7. Pursuit

### 7.1 Demands

A demand states the facts, the legal theory in plain language, the amount paid, the deductible treatment, and the documents enclosed. It gives a reasonable response date, usually twenty-one days. It is sent to the party and to any known insurer. Demands above $50,000, and any demand that will be followed by suit, are approved by the Recovery manager. Demands above $150,000, and any demand that alleges a novel theory, are approved by Legal before they are sent.

The amount demanded is the amount the law allows Northline to recover. Inflating a demand to create bargaining room beyond a good-faith figure is not permitted. A later reduction based on new facts is ordinary negotiation. A reduction because the first number was never supportable is a defect.

### 7.2 Negotiation and suit

Recovery may settle within its authority table. The current limits are $15,000 for a Recovery specialist, $50,000 for a Recovery supervisor, and $150,000 for the Head of Recovery. Above that, a claim manager and Legal concur. Settlements are documented with a release that names the claim, the amount, and the parties, and that does not admit Northline's insured was at fault unless that admission is the Company's position and the insured has been consulted as CLM-005 requires.

Suit is filed only when Legal agrees the liability theory is supportable, the collection prospect justifies the cost, and the venue is proper. The statute of limitations is diaried at the start of the pursuit, not in the month it expires. A missed limitation is reported to the Head of Recovery the day it is discovered, with the amount lost. It is not quietly closed.

### 7.3 When to stop

Pursuit is closed without recovery when:

- the expected net recovery is below the threshold and no deductible-return purpose justifies a limited demand;
- liability evidence is insufficient and further investigation is unlikely to repair it;
- the defendant cannot pay and no insurer responds, after a reasonable asset or coverage check proportionate to the amount;
- the insured's defense or ongoing relationship under a waiver endorsement would be harmed;
- Legal advises that the theory is not viable in the jurisdiction.

The closure note states the reason and the amount abandoned. Abandoned amounts are not written off in a batch at year-end with no file-level reason.

## 8. Deductibles, made whole, and distribution

Every recovery is distributed according to the state addendum. The file shows the gross recovery, collection costs allowed by the addendum, the insured's share, the deductible returned, and the Company's share. In a made-whole state, the insured's uninsured loss, including the deductible if the addendum includes it, is addressed before the Company retains money. In a pro-rata state, the split follows the addendum's formula. Staff do not keep the entire recovery and invite the insured to ask for the deductible later. The deductible refund, if due, is issued within fifteen business days of the recovery posting.

If the insured has a lawyer pursuing the same defendant for amounts Northline did not pay, Recovery coordinates so the defendant is not subjected to inconsistent demands and so the insured's recovery is not undercut. Cost sharing with the insured's lawyer follows the addendum and any written agreement Legal approves. Northline does not refuse to cooperate with the insured's lawyer to improve the Company's percentage.

Money that belongs to the insured is not used to offset a premium balance unless the insured agrees in writing or the law allows the offset and Billing has confirmed it. The default is a check or electronic payment to the insured for the insured's share.

## 9. Salvage

### 9.1 When title transfers

On a total-loss vehicle, title is transferred as the state motor-vehicle rules and the addendum require, after the customer has been paid or the payment has been tendered according to CLM-005. Northline does not withhold an agreed total-loss payment to force a faster title. On real or personal property, the policy's abandonment and salvage clause is followed. The Company does not take salvage it does not want. If the Company declines salvage, the customer is told in writing that they may keep the property and the payment is reduced only if the policy and the valuation method call for a deduction. The deduction is explained in dollars.

### 9.2 Sale

Salvage is sold through approved salvage vendors or auctions. The sale record shows the property, the buyer, the gross price, and the fees. Employees, agents, and their households may not purchase. A vendor that handles the salvage may not buy it except through a blind auction the vendor does not run. Proceeds are applied to the claim under the reserve method in CLM-004. Until the sale, the expected salvage is reserved only as that policy allows.

Owner-retained salvage is allowed when the addendum permits and the customer accepts a documented credit. The credit is based on the salvage vendor's figure or another supportable market figure, not on a number chosen to close the file faster. Unsafe salvage — contaminated food, structurally dangerous items sold for habitable use, vehicles that should be branded — is handled as the law requires and is not sold into a use that hides the brand or the hazard.

### 9.3 Storage

Storage charges are reviewed monthly on open salvage. A vehicle or machine sitting in paid storage after the inspection hold has expired is a recovery failure. The Head of Recovery receives a report of salvage in storage more than forty-five days.

## 10. Contribution between insurers

Contribution files are pursued on the policy language, not on a custom of "splitting it." The other carrier receives the tender, the forms relied on, and the payment proofs. If both policies claim to be excess, CLM-002's counsel path is used before anyone agrees to a split that the forms do not support. Agreements to share defense or indemnity are in writing. A phone agreement is confirmed by email the same day and is not treated as final until the writing exists.

Northline answers incoming contribution demands with the same care. A demand against Northline is logged as a claim event under CLM-001 if it alleges Northline's policy owes a payment, even if Northline already has a file. Ignoring an incoming tender because "we already closed our insured's claim" can forfeit a defense. The examiner responds within the time the tender states, or within fifteen business days if it states none.

## 11. Arbitration and inter-company agreements

Where Northline is a signatory to an inter-company arbitration agreement that covers the dispute, Recovery uses that forum instead of suit if the agreement requires it. Filings meet the forum's evidence rules. Awards are paid or pursued as the award states. Staff do not refuse to honor an adverse award because they disagree with it. They may seek whatever review the forum allows, with Legal's approval, and they diary the review deadline.

A customer is not forced into that inter-company forum for their own uninsured loss. The forum decides disputes between members. The customer's deductible distribution still follows Section 8 after the award.

## 12. Accounting and reserves

A recovery reserve, or an expected-recovery code if the bulletin uses a code instead of a negative reserve, is posted when payment is made and the opportunity is more likely than not. It is not posted at intake on a hope. It is revised when liability or collectibility changes. Received funds are identified to the claim within five business days of receipt. Unidentified funds are escalated to Claim Accounting the day they are noticed. A recovery is not left in a suspense account because the file is closed. Closed files are reopened under CLM-001 to receive the money and to refund a deductible.

Charge-off of an expected recovery is approved at the same authority level that would settle it. The reason is one of the stop reasons in Section 7.3.

## 13. Conduct toward third parties

Recovery staff state who they are and whom they represent. They do not imply they are a regulator, a lawyer for the debtor, or a credit bureau. They do not contact a represented party directly. They accept reasonable installment plans when the amount and the person's circumstances make a lump sum unrealistic, and they confirm the plan in writing. Harassment — repeated calls after a written cease request that still leaves a lawful channel open, calls to a workplace that the law protects, or threats the Company cannot carry out — is a violation. A debtor's complaint is handled under CLM-009 even though the person is not Northline's insured.

## 14. Quality, training, and exceptions

Quality Review samples recovery screens on closed paid files and samples demands above $10,000. A file fails if a clear third-party cause was ignored, if a waiver endorsement was overlooked, if a deductible due back to the insured was kept, if salvage was sold to a prohibited buyer, or if a limitation date was missed. The Head of Recovery reports recovered dollars, abandoned dollars, and missed limitations to the Claims Governance Committee each quarter. The report is not a quota. Staff are not rewarded for demands that are later withdrawn as unsupported.

New Recovery staff do not send demands until they have completed the subrogation module and the made-whole module for the states they will handle. Adjusters complete a shorter module on the recovery screen and on releases. Annual training covers one waiver-endorsement example and one deductible-distribution example.

A CAT bulletin may delay salvage pickup and may authorize a simplified recovery screen for the first thirty days. It may not waive limitation diaries or waiver-endorsement checks. There is no exception that lets an employee buy salvage. There is no exception that lets Recovery direct an insured's liability defense.

## 15. Related policies

CLM-001 creates the event number used to link clash recoveries. CLM-002 identifies other insurance and waiver endorsements. CLM-003 preserves the physical evidence. CLM-004 states when expected salvage is netted. CLM-005 governs the insured's payment, which usually happens before recovery and is not reduced by a speculative future recovery. CLM-006 is not the path for telling a third party it owes subrogation; a demand is. CLM-007 handles fraud restitution separately. CLM-009 handles disputes about distribution. CLM-010 coordinates mass salvage after a catastrophe so storage and auctions are arranged before the loss is paid, when the bulletin says so.

## Appendix A — Examples

**Contractor-caused water loss.** A roofer leaves a deck uncovered and rain damages the insured's ceiling. Northline pays the ceiling under the homeowners policy. The recovery screen names the roofer, attaches the contract and photos, and confirms there is no waiver-of-subrogation endorsement. Recovery sends a preservation notice and, after payment, a demand for the amount paid plus the deductible treatment the addendum requires. The insured is not told to sue the roofer instead of using their policy.

**Waiver endorsement.** The same facts, except the policy carries a waiver of subrogation in favor of parties the insured agreed to waive in a written construction contract, and the contract with the roofer contains that waiver. Recovery closes the roofer as a target and notes the endorsement and the contract. No demand goes out. The insured's claim is unaffected.

**Deductible arithmetic.** Northline paid $9,000 and the insured absorbed a $1,000 deductible. The state is a made-whole state under the addendum, and the insured has no uninsured loss other than the deductible. Recovery obtains $7,000. Collection cost allowed by the addendum is $350. The net is distributed so the deductible is addressed under the addendum's made-whole rule before Northline treats the remainder as its own. The arithmetic is in the file and the insured's check, if one is due, goes out within fifteen business days. A note that says "company keeps $7,000, insured already had a deductible" is wrong in that state.

**Do not hold the customer's check.** The auto evaluation is agreed. Subrogation against the other driver looks likely but has not been collected. The customer's collision payment is issued under CLM-005. It is not reduced by the expected recovery. When recovery arrives, the deductible is refunded if the distribution requires it.

**Salvage timing.** A total-loss vehicle is also needed for a liability photo inspection because fault is disputed and the other carrier has asked to see it. Recovery holds the vehicle, tells the salvage vendor the hold will be no more than thirty days, completes the inspection, and then releases the vehicle. The customer's payment is not delayed for the hold. Title transfer follows the payment under the addendum.

**Incoming tender ignored.** Northline closed its insured's first-party file. Two months later the other driver's insurer tenders a liability claim against Northline's insured. The closer's instinct is to say the file is closed. That is wrong. The tender is logged and answered under Section 10. The liability question is investigated. The first-party closure did not decide the third-party claim.

**Below the threshold.** A third party scuffed a fence. Northline's payment above the deductible was $640. Collection cost would exceed the recovery. There is no product issue and no insurer to tender to. Recovery closes the opportunity under the threshold and still refunds nothing because nothing was collected. The screen explains the closure. The adjuster does not send a threatening letter "on principle."

## Appendix B — Recovery-screen checklist

- [ ] Possible third parties are named, or "none" is explained.
- [ ] Waiver-of-subrogation endorsements and relevant contracts were checked before any demand.
- [ ] Evidence holds are in place or deliberately released.
- [ ] Another insurer has been tendered, or ruled out.
- [ ] The statute of limitations is diaried if a claim will be pursued.
- [ ] The demand amount matches what was paid and what the law allows.
- [ ] Authority for the demand or suit is documented.
- [ ] Distribution math, including the deductible, is ready before money arrives.
- [ ] Salvage is either declined in writing, sold through an approved channel, or owner-retained with a supported credit.
- [ ] No employee, agent, or household member is the buyer.
- [ ] Incoming tenders have a response date.
- [ ] Closure of a pursuit uses a Section 7.3 reason.
- [ ] Funds are applied to the claim and are not sitting in suspense.
