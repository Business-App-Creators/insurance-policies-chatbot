## 1. Purpose

A catastrophe is a surge of claims from one event, large enough that ordinary staffing, ordinary inspection timing, and ordinary vendor capacity will not carry it. A large loss is a single claim whose size, injuries, or complexity would overwhelm a standard desk even if it happened alone. This policy tells Northline how to declare those situations, how authority and timelines change while they last, and which rules do not change at all. The coverage test in CLM-002, the evidence rules in CLM-003, the reserve honesty rules in CLM-004, the payee rules in CLM-005, the denial rules in CLM-006, and the complaint rules in CLM-009 remain in force. A catastrophe is not a suspension of the contract the customer bought.

The operational goal is simple to say and hard to do: people who cannot live in their homes or operate their businesses get a fast, accurate response, and the Company does not pay the wrong cause of loss because the queue is long. Speed is planned in advance so it does not depend on heroics the week the storm arrives.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies when the Chief Claims Officer or a designated deputy declares a catastrophe event, and when any file meets the large-loss definition whether or not a catastrophe is open. It binds employees, independent adjusters appointed for the event, and firms on the catastrophe vendor panel. It does not, by itself, change reinsurance treaties. Reinsurance notices required by those treaties are still made, using the facts this policy's reporting section produces.

A busy Friday, a local hail cell with forty claims, or a single commercial fire below the large-loss threshold is handled with ordinary tools plus a supervisor's workload plan. Those events are not given a catastrophe code to avoid ordinary timelines. Mis-coding is a reporting violation because it pollutes the Company's catastrophe data and the customer's deductible treatment if a percentage deductible is wrongly triggered.

## 3. Definitions

**Catastrophe event (CAT).** A declared event with a CAT code, a named peril, a geography, a start time, and an expected end of new losses. The code is what attaches a claim to the event. A claim is not a CAT claim because the customer used the word "storm."

**CAT bulletin.** The written instruction issued for that event. It is the only place ordinary timelines, desk-adjustment permission, advance amounts, and price-list deviations may be changed. Verbal instructions in a briefing are confirmed in the bulletin the same day or they expire.

**Large loss.** A file with an indemnity reserve of $250,000 or more, a fatality, a suit seeking extra-contractual damages, a commercial loss that stops a business above the reserve threshold, or any file the large-loss unit accepts because of complexity. The threshold matches the reinsurance notice line in CLM-004 so the two systems do not diverge.

**Desk adjustment.** A decision based on photos, aerial imagery, invoices, and statements, without a visit, allowed only when the bulletin or CLM-003 permits it and the photo standard is met.

**Make-safe.** Temporary work to protect property from further damage: tarping, board-up, water extraction, temporary power or fencing when needed for safety. It is not the permanent repair.

**Advance.** A payment of a stated amount before the full estimate, credited to the final payment, allowed only as the bulletin or CLM-005 describes.

**Deployed adjuster.** A staff or independent adjuster assigned outside their home territory for the event, working under the bulletin and a Northline examiner.

## 4. Governing principles

1. Declare early, in writing, with a code and a map. An undeclared emergency produces undeclared exceptions.
2. The bulletin may relax a Company timeline. It may not relax a statute, a payment duty on an undisputed amount, or a safety duty.
3. Cause of loss is still required. Wind, flood, storm surge, and wear are not lumped into one payable pile because the neighborhood was hit hard.
4. Customers hear from a person who has the file. Mass emails do not replace the acknowledgment and status duties except as the bulletin expressly allows and the law permits.
5. Vendors are on the panel or are approved for the event in writing. A truck that arrives uninvited is not given a Northline assignment because it is convenient.
6. Large-loss files get a named examiner and a second set of eyes. They are not left in the CAT queue because the event is busy.
7. When the event is over, the code is closed, the exceptions expire, and files return to ordinary handling. Exceptions do not linger out of habit.

## 5. Declaration and the bulletin

### 5.1 Who declares

The Chief Claims Officer declares a CAT after notice from weather services, claim-volume monitoring, or civil authorities that a peril will produce, or is producing, losses beyond ordinary capacity. A deputy designated in writing may declare if the Chief Claims Officer is unreachable, and the Chief Claims Officer confirms or ends the declaration within twenty-four hours. The declaration names the peril, the states or counties, the start time, the CAT code, and the initial desk that will run operations.

A declaration is not a statement that every loss in the area is covered. Communications staff are told that distinction before any public message goes out. Public messages tell customers how to report, how to perform make-safe work, and how to reach Northline. They do not say "if you had damage, you are covered."

### 5.2 What the bulletin may change

The first bulletin is issued the day of the declaration and updated as facts change. It may:

- set acknowledgment and inspection times that differ from CLM-001 and CLM-003, within the law;
- authorize desk adjustment for specified perils and size bands if the photo standard in the bulletin is met;
- authorize a make-safe advance up to a stated dollar amount before inspection;
- authorize a temporary price-list adjustment when local labor or materials have genuinely moved, with an end date;
- name the vendor panel, the firms allowed to tarp or extract, and the fee schedule;
- set lodging, mileage, and authority for deployed adjusters;
- assign extra denial approvers under CLM-006 without lowering the content of a denial;
- extend SIU's first-assessment time under CLM-007, without allowing a silent payment hold.

The bulletin may not:

- instruct staff to pay flood on a policy that excludes flood;
- waive mortgagee payees, minor-payee rules, or proof of loss where the Company intends to rely on the condition;
- permit secret recordings, pretext investigations, or a lowering of reserves to manage the financial result;
- close appeals or lengthen a statutory complaint deadline;
- authorize off-panel vendors except through the written exception in Section 9.

Each bulletin version is numbered. Staff follow the current version. Old versions stay in the event file so a later audit can see what rule was in force on a given day.

### 5.3 Closing the event

The Chief Claims Officer closes the CAT code when new notices have returned to a volume the ordinary desks can take and the fieldwork backlog is assigned. Closure is written. After closure, new claims from the same storm are still coded to the event if the date of loss falls inside the event window, but they use ordinary timelines unless the bulletin's tail section says otherwise. The tail section has an end date. Open files do not keep CAT exceptions indefinitely.

## 6. Intake during a catastrophe

The First Notice Desk uses the CAT script from CLM-001. The minimum data set still applies. In addition, the opener records whether the dwelling is habitable, whether anyone is injured, whether a tree or power line is creating danger, and whether the customer needs the make-safe advance. Duplicate searches are not skipped. A second claim for the same house and the same storm is a supplement or a duplicate, not a new event, unless a second policy is involved.

If phone lines are saturated, the bulletin may direct customers to the portal and may staff an overflow vendor whose scripts Northline has approved. The vendor opens files in NCF or in a bridge system that is loaded into NCF the same night. A spreadsheet that is not loaded is not a claim file. The acknowledgment still goes out within the bulletin's time, with a claim number, a contact path, and make-safe instructions. Customers are told to keep receipts and to photograph damage before temporary repairs if they can do so safely.

Agents in the affected area may be unable to forward notices the same day. The bulletin may give agents a short, stated extension and must still treat notice to the agent as notice to the Company under CLM-001. Customers are not charged with the agent's outage.

## 7. Field operations

### 7.1 Deployment

The operations lead assigns geography, licenses, and lodging before adjusters travel. An adjuster who is not licensed where the state requires a license does not inspect there. Deployed staff receive the bulletin, the price list, the photo standard, and a safety briefing. They do not begin inspections until that briefing is done. Daily check-in is mandatory. An adjuster who misses check-in is contacted. If they cannot be reached and a safety concern exists, the operations lead escalates to local emergency contacts. Production targets are secondary to the safety briefing's rules on roofs, standing water, curfews, and heat.

### 7.2 What the visit is for

The inspection note meets CLM-003. In a catastrophe the note also records flood line or water height if water is alleged, wind direction indicators the adjuster actually sees, roof covering and age if known, and whether damage is clearly older than the event. "Storm damage throughout" is not a note. Where the bulletin allows desk adjustment, the photo set must show each elevation, the roof if safely photographed by the customer or a drone vendor, interior stains with a wide shot that locates them, and a photo of the front of the risk for identification. If the photos are not enough to separate causes, a visit is scheduled. Desk adjustment is a permission, not a quota.

### 7.3 Habitability and additional living expense

If the home is not safely habitable for a covered cause, the adjuster authorizes additional living expense within the policy and within their authority the day of the inspection, or the day the photos prove it on a desk file. The customer is told the time limit and the dollar limit in writing, in the same week, not after the first hotel bill is rejected. Advances for short-term lodging follow the bulletin. Evacuation orders alone do not create additional living expense if the policy requires a covered loss to the dwelling. The letter says so plainly when that is the decision, and a denial of living expense is still a denial under CLM-006.

## 8. Coverage in a mixed event

Wind, hail, flood, storm surge, sewer backup, and tree fall can happen in one weekend and damage one house. The adjuster separates them. The screen cites the facts for each. Flood and storm surge are not paid under a homeowners policy that excludes them because a CAT was declared. A flood policy, if Northline writes one on the same property, is a second claim number under the same event, handled on that form. Wind damage above a flood line can be covered while damage below it is not. The estimate is split. A single estimate that calls everything "hurricane" and pays it all is a defect. A single estimate that denies everything "because of flooding" without looking at the wind damage above the line is also a defect.

Percentage wind or named-storm deductibles are applied only when the declarations and the event meet the form's definition. The bulletin may remind staff of the definition. It may not create a deductible the policy does not have, and it may not waive one the policy does have, except where a regulator's binding order requires a waiver and Compliance confirms the order in the bulletin. The arithmetic is shown on each file, as CLM-002 requires.

Matching, ordinance or law, and ensuing-loss questions are decided under CLM-002. CAT volume does not create a shortcut denial and does not create a shortcut payment of code items that were not triggered.

## 9. Vendors, pricing, and fraud pressure

The panel is chosen before storm season and confirmed in the bulletin. Fees, documentation standards, and the rule against assignment of benefits, if the policy prohibits it, are in the vendor contract. A vendor that solicits Northline customers with a promise that the deductible will be waived or that the roof will be "covered by insurance" is reported to Vendor Management the day staff learn of it. Northline does not waive a deductible to match a contractor's promise. Staff warn the customer, plainly, that the deductible is the customer's share and that a contractor who offers to hide it is asking them to participate in a false invoice. That warning is given without accusing the customer of already agreeing.

Price-list increases are allowed only under the bulletin, with a reason and an end date. An adjuster who meets a genuinely higher local price writes the variance on the estimate. A general "CAT factor" typed into every file is not allowed. Supplements are audited. After the event, Vendor Management samples invoices against photos. Systematic overbilling is referred under CLM-007's vendor section and is pursued as a vendor-audit recovery. It is not ignored because the vendor was helpful in the first week.

Off-panel firms are used only when the panel cannot reach a geography and a manager approves the firm in writing for a stated period. The firm receives the bulletin's documentation rules before the first assignment. Firms that will not document to that standard are not hired.

## 10. Large-loss files

### 10.1 Acceptance

The large-loss unit reviews every file that crosses the threshold the day it crosses, and every fatality the day it is reported. A CAT file that is also a large loss is dual-tracked: the CAT code remains for event reporting, and the large-loss examiner owns the strategy, the reserve, and the coverage screen. The file is not left with a deployed independent adjuster as the final decision maker.

### 10.2 Team and plan

Within three business days of acceptance, the examiner writes a plan: cause experts, counsel if a suit or a coverage question requires it, a reserve path, a customer-communication owner, and a reinsurance notice. The plan identifies what could move the number by a material amount. On a commercial property loss that stops operations, the plan includes business-interruption documents, a mitigation conversation with the insured, and a diary for the extra-expense and period-of-restoration issues. The insured is given one primary contact. They are not asked to repeat the loss story to a new person each week unless the contact changes for a stated reason, in which case the new contact reads the file first.

### 10.3 Fatalities and serious injuries

A fatality or a serious bodily-injury CAT claim is referred to the casualty large-loss examiner even if the property damage is small. Contact with the family is respectful, limited to what the claim requires, and coordinated with counsel if liability is in question. Staff do not request an examination under oath of a grieving family as an early step. Reserves follow CLM-004, including a realistic severity based on the facts then known, not a token. Communications about the event go through Communications. The examiner does not speak to the press.

## 11. Payments, complaints, and recovery in the surge

Advances authorized by the bulletin are paid to the correct payees under CLM-005, credited on the final estimate, and not described as a final settlement. Undisputed portions of a split wind-and-flood loss are paid when the wind portion is measured. They are not held until every coverage question on the property is closed, unless the open question could erase the wind payment itself and that possibility is documented.

Complaint volume rises after a CAT. CLM-009 stays in force. The bulletin may add Customer Relations staff and publish a temporary contact. It may not authorize a script that tells people their appeal will have to wait until next season. Regulatory deadlines are kept.

Recovery and salvage under CLM-008 are organized for the event: a salvage staging plan, a hold on vehicles and equipment needed for subrogation, and a clash list of losses caused by one contractor or one product. The event number is the key. Individual files still receive their own recovery screen before closure. A CAT is not a reason to skip subrogation against a contractor whose work failed in the storm, and it is not a reason to pursue neighbors who were equally victims of the weather.

## 12. Communication

The bulletin names a Communications owner. Customer-facing messages are approved by that owner and by Claims for accuracy. Messages include how to file, what make-safe work is reimbursable if the loss is covered, what photos help, and a warning about uninvited contractors and deductible-waiver schemes. Messages do not estimate how fast every roof will be repaired and do not promise coverage.

Internal rumors are corrected in the next bulletin. Adjusters do not tell customers "everyone is getting a new roof" or "nothing is being paid until the governor announces something." If a governmental order changes a duty, Compliance writes the effect into the bulletin and staff quote that, not a headline.

Customers who cannot return home, who have lost phones, or who are in shelters are given flexible contact methods. A letter sent only to a destroyed house does not count as notice if the file shows the house is uninhabitable and another channel exists. The adjuster records the channel that worked.

## 13. Money, data, and reinsurance

Finance and Claims reconcile CAT payments weekly during the event: advances outstanding, indemnity by peril, expense, and vendor accruals. The reconciliation is an internal control. It is not a target that adjusters are told to hit. Reserves on CAT files follow CLM-004. A simplified intake reserve may be used for seventy-two hours if the bulletin says so. After inspection, the evaluated reserve replaces it. Large-loss and treaty notices go out under CLM-004 the day a threshold is crossed. Reinsurance reporting uses the CAT code so a clash is visible. Adjusters do not call reinsurers.

After closure of the code, the Reserve Committee reviews development on the event at ninety days and again at the next quarterly reserve study. Persistent under-reserving at first inspection is corrected in the method, as CLM-004 describes, not by a speech about leakage.

## 14. Safety, conduct, and fatigue

Deployed staff are told the maximum consecutive workdays in the bulletin. The operations lead sends people home or to rest when the limit is reached or when fatigue makes field work unsafe. Production numbers are not a reason to extend the limit. Alcohol during the workday, inspections conducted under the influence, and harassment of customers, residents, or coworkers are grounds for immediate removal from the event and for disciplinary action. Local curfews and exclusion zones are obeyed. An adjuster turned away by emergency management does not sneak into a neighborhood to finish a roof count.

Customers in crisis are spoken to with patience. A person who has lost a home may be angry or disorganized. That is not an indicator under CLM-007. A false invoice scheme is an indicator. The difference is the facts, and staff are trained on it before deployment.

## 15. Quality during and after the event

During the event, Quality Review samples desk adjustments, split cause-of-loss estimates, advances, and habitability decisions in near real time, not only after the code closes. A failed desk adjustment — photos that do not support the cause, or a flood item paid as wind — is corrected on that file and the estimator is coached before they write the next ten estimates. After the event, a larger sample is scored for deductible accuracy, mortgagee payees, unsupported line items, and timelines. Results are reported to the Claims Governance Committee with the event's complaint and reopening rates. Independent adjusters with poor scores are not reappointed to the next event until they are retrained. Firms with systematic overbilling are removed from the panel.

Employees are not punished for a slow week caused by access restrictions, and they are not rewarded for estimate totals. The score is accuracy, safety, and kept promises.

## 16. Training and pre-season readiness

Before the season, Claims runs a readiness review: panel contracts current, licenses mapped to likely states, bulletin template updated, intake scripts loaded, lodging process tested, and the large-loss on-call list confirmed. Gaps are assigned owners and dates. A readiness review that produces no changes in a year when the prior event's audit found defects is not a serious review.

Staff who will deploy complete the CAT module: split causes, deductible types, desk-photo standards, advances, and safety. Independent firms complete it before they receive a code. A firm that shows up without the module is not assigned files. Annual training uses one redacted mixed wind-and-flood file and one commercial interruption file so large-loss examiners and CAT adjusters have seen the same facts from both procedures.

## 17. Exceptions

The only operational exceptions to ordinary claim rules during a catastrophe are the ones written in the current bulletin and allowed by Section 5.2. A field briefing does not add exceptions. A large-loss file may receive more resources than this policy requires. It may not receive fewer controls. If NCF fails during an event, the outage procedure in CLM-001 applies, the operations lead assigns a paper-control owner, and the backlog is keyed in priority order: uninhabitable dwellings, injuries, and then other claims. Payments already promised are reconciled first so duplicate checks do not go out when the system returns.

## 18. Related policies

CLM-001's catastrophe intake section is the front door. CLM-002 is the coverage test the bulletin cannot rewrite. CLM-003 is the inspection standard, including preservation of evidence. CLM-004 is the reserve method and the large-loss notice line. CLM-005 is how advances and final payments are made. CLM-006 is how any denial, including a living-expense or flood denial, is issued. CLM-007 is the referral path for invoice schemes and is not the path for ordinary storm claims. CLM-008 is salvage, clash, and contractor recovery. CLM-009 is the complaint path during and after the surge.

## Appendix A — Examples

**Declaration.** A hurricane is forecast to cross two states where Northline writes homeowners and commercial property. The Chief Claims Officer declares the event the day before landfall, assigns a code, and issues a bulletin that allows a $2,500 make-safe advance, sets a photo standard for desk adjustment of minor exterior losses, and names the tarp panel. The public message tells customers how to board up and how to file. It does not say all hurricane damage is covered. Claims inside the geography with a different date of loss are not given the code.

**Split causes.** A house has a water line four feet up the wall and missing shingles on the south slope. The policy excludes flood and covers wind. The estimate pays the wind-damaged roof and the interior damage that the facts show came from rain through the wind opening. It does not pay the lower four feet of drywall if the facts show flood. The letter itemizes both decisions. A one-line denial of the entire claim "due to flooding" is returned by quality review. A one-line payment of the entire contractor invoice is also returned.

**Desk adjustment refused by the facts.** The customer uploads a photo of a wet ceiling and a distant shot of the roof. The bulletin allows desk adjustment only when elevations and the roof are shown well enough to separate wind from wear. The adjuster schedules a visit or a drone inspection. The file is not paid as wind, and it is not denied as wear, on the weak photo set.

**Advance and mortgagee.** The bulletin allows a $2,500 make-safe advance. The dwelling has a mortgagee. Under CLM-005 and this bulletin, an advance of that size for temporary tarping may be paid to the insured or the approved tarp vendor with the insured's direction, and the bulletin says which. A permanent roof payment of $18,000 still names the mortgagee. The advance is credited. The customer is not told the advance was "extra."

**Contractor at the curb.** A contractor tells the insured the deductible will be "taken care of" if the insured signs an assignment. The adjuster tells the insured the deductible will be applied, that Northline pays from its own estimate, and that a false deductible waiver on an invoice is not something the Company will honor. The contractor is reported to Vendor Management. The insured is not referred to SIU because a contractor made an offer. A referral happens if a false invoice is actually submitted and the insured adopts it after a plain question, under CLM-007.

**Large loss inside a CAT.** A cold-storage facility loses its roof and its inventory in the same hurricane. The reserve crosses $250,000 the first day. The large-loss unit takes the file that day, assigns one contact, hires a cause-and-origin expert and an accountant under CLM-003, notices Reinsurance, and leaves the CAT code in place for aggregation. The deployed roof adjuster may measure the roof. The deployed adjuster does not settle the inventory or the business interruption.

**Fatigue.** An adjuster has worked the bulletin's maximum days and says they can "do one more street." The operations lead says no and reassigns the street. The decision is not a performance mark against the adjuster. An adjuster who continues after being stood down is removed from the deployment.

**End of exceptions.** The code is closed on 1 October. A supplement on a coded claim arrives on 20 October. The tail section ended on 15 October. The supplement is handled on the original claim under ordinary CLM-003 and CLM-004 timelines. The adjuster does not apply the expired desk-adjustment shortcut. A new hail loss on 20 October is a new claim and is not given the hurricane code.

## Appendix B — Bulletin checklist

Use this list before a bulletin is issued or revised.

- [ ] The code, peril, geography, and start time are stated.
- [ ] The version number and date are on the first page.
- [ ] Any change to acknowledgment or inspection time cites the legal limit it stays inside.
- [ ] Desk adjustment, if allowed, names the photo standard and the size band.
- [ ] Advances, if allowed, name the amount, the payee rule, and the credit to the final estimate.
- [ ] Price-list changes have a reason and an end date.
- [ ] The vendor panel is named, and off-panel use requires a written manager approval.
- [ ] Deductible treatment is a reminder of the forms, not a new deductible.
- [ ] Flood and wind are required to be separated.
- [ ] Denial content, mortgagee rules, and complaint deadlines are expressly unchanged.
- [ ] SIU timing, if changed, still requires a customer letter before a long hold.
- [ ] Fatigue and safety limits are included.
- [ ] Large-loss dual tracking is stated for files that cross the threshold.
- [ ] Reinsurance notice is assigned to a named role.
- [ ] The tail, or the lack of a tail, has an end date.
- [ ] Communications has approved customer-facing language, and that language does not promise coverage.
- [ ] The prior version is retained in the event file.
