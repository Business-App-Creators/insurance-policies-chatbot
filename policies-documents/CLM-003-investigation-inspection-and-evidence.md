## 1. Purpose

Coverage and payment depend on facts the Company can explain. This policy states how those facts are gathered: site inspections, photographs, recorded statements, documents, experts, medical examinations, and examinations under oath. It also states what staff may not do in the name of investigation. An investigation is finished when the remaining unknown facts are unlikely to change the coverage result or the dollar amount, not when every imaginable record has been collected.

Northline investigates claims it has opened under CLM-001. It does not investigate people for reasons unrelated to the claim. It does not use the claim file as a reason to enter a home beyond the loss, to record a person secretly, or to contact a represented person directly. Fraud indicators are referred under CLM-007. Ordinary investigation stays with the adjuster.

## 2. Scope

The assigned adjuster owns the investigation plan. Independent adjusters and experts work inside that plan and inside their assignment letters. Defense counsel owns investigation of litigated liability once counsel is assigned, and coordinates with the adjuster so the claim file and the litigation file do not diverge. A fact counsel learns that affects coverage, reserves, or injury value is sent to the adjuster the week it is learned, subject to any privilege note counsel places on the message.

This policy covers first-party property, first-party auto, and third-party liability. Workers' compensation, if ever written by a Northline affiliate, is outside this manual. Health records are requested only when the claim puts the person's medical condition in issue, and only with the authorization or legal process Section 8 requires.

## 3. Definitions

**Examination under oath (EUO).** A formal statement taken under the policy's oath condition, with a transcript, after notice of the topics and after the person has had a fair chance to attend with a representative. It is not a recorded telephone interview.

**Expert.** A person retained for a technical opinion the adjuster is not qualified to give: cause and origin, engineering, building code, accident reconstruction, medical specialty review, or a forensic accountant on a business-interruption loss. A contractor who writes an estimate is a vendor under the repair program, not an expert, unless retained for a cause opinion.

**Independent medical examination (IME).** An examination by a physician Northline retains, used on a bodily-injury claim when the treating records are not enough to evaluate the injury the claimant has put in issue. It is not a treatment relationship.

**Inspection.** A first-hand look at the damaged property, vehicle, or scene, done by an adjuster or by a person the assignment letter authorizes, with photos and measurements stored in NCF.

**Recorded statement.** An audio statement taken with notice to the speaker that it is being recorded, where the law allows recording with that notice. A surprise recording is not a recorded statement under this policy. It is a violation of it.

**Spoliation.** The loss or destruction of evidence that a person had a duty to keep. The Company can commit it by failing to preserve a vehicle, a part, or a file, and an insured or claimant can commit it by discarding property after being told to keep it. The response is a documented prejudice analysis, not an automatic denial.

## 4. Governing principles

1. Ask for the evidence that can change the result. A long list sent to look thorough is a delay.
2. See the property when sight would change cause or cost, unless a documented exception in Section 6 applies.
3. The file distinguishes observation from conclusion. "The shingles are creased across the south slope" is an observation. "This is hail from the April storm" is a conclusion and needs the reasons.
4. People are told why a statement, document, or examination is being requested and what policy term, if any, requires it.
5. One missed appointment is rescheduled. Noncooperation is a pattern plus prejudice, analyzed under CLM-002, not a label applied after a single no-show.
6. Evidence is stored in NCF. Photos on a phone that never upload are treated as if they do not exist, and the adjuster who lost them owns the recovery effort.
7. Investigation respects the home. Adjusters do not open drawers unrelated to the loss, comment on housekeeping except as it bears on cause, or bring an unexpected second person into a home without telling the occupant who that person is and why they are there.

## 5. The investigation plan

Within two business days of assignment on a standard file, and the same day on a fatality, an uninhabitable dwelling, a large-loss file, or a defense tender, the adjuster writes a short plan in NCF:

- the decisions the file still needs (cause, scope, injury, liability, identity of insured);
- the evidence that will support those decisions;
- who will collect it and by when;
- any evidence at risk of disappearing, and the preservation step.

The plan is updated when a new theory appears. It is not a script the adjuster must exhaust if an earlier step already answered the question. A kitchen-supply-line break with clear photos, a plumber's invoice, and an agreement on price does not need an engineer. A collapsed roof does.

Preservation comes first when the evidence can disappear. Examples: a vehicle about to be repaired, a pipe fitting the plumber intends to discard, security video that a store overwrites in seven days, and a commercial machine that will be put back into service. The adjuster sends a written hold the day the risk is known, identifying the item and asking that it be kept until inspection or until the adjuster releases it in writing. The hold is specific. "Keep everything related to the claim" is not a useful hold and is not used on consumer files. It may be used, more specifically detailed, on a commercial loss with counsel's help.

## 6. Inspections

### 6.1 When a site visit is required

A physical inspection is required before a property or auto physical-damage payment, unless one of these exceptions is documented:

- the amount payable is at or below the desk-authority threshold published in the current payment bulletin, the photos and invoices are enough to measure the loss, and the cause is not in real dispute;
- the property has been demolished or the vehicle sold, and the file explains what secondary evidence was used;
- a catastrophe bulletin under CLM-010 authorizes desk adjustment for that peril and geography, and the file meets the bulletin's photo standard;
- the insured refuses access after two written requests, in which case the file proceeds on the evidence available and records the refusal for a cooperation analysis.

Liability scenes are inspected when a view would affect fault or notice of a hazard: a fall on a step, a lighting claim, a commercial parking lot, a vehicle with disputed damage. A minor parking-lot door ding with consistent photos does not need a site visit if the drivers agree on the event.

### 6.2 Scheduling and conduct

The adjuster offers two appointment windows within five business days of assignment for local files, or within the CAT bulletin's time for catastrophe files. The occupant is told who will attend. Inspections are done during reasonable hours unless the loss is an emergency make-safe. The adjuster arrives with identification. They do not enter if the only person present is a minor. They do not comment to neighbors about the insured's claim. Measurements, roof access, and moisture readings follow the safety rules in the field manual. No adjuster walks a roof they are not equipped and authorized to walk. Drone or ladder-assist vendors are used instead, and the limitation is noted so a later reviewer knows the roof was seen, and how.

### 6.3 What the inspection note contains

The note is written the day of the inspection or the next business day. It includes arrival time, who was present, weather if it matters, a room-by-room or elevation-by-elevation description, measurements, materials, cause observations, temporary repairs already done, habitability, and a list of photos with what each photo shows. "See photos" is not a note. The note also records what the occupant said on site, labeled as their statement, and any disagreement about scope. If the adjuster cannot determine cause, the note says so and recommends the expert. It does not fill the gap with a guess.

Photos must show the overall scene, the specific damage, a reference for scale, and any pre-existing condition the estimate will exclude. Metadata is preserved. Photos are not edited except for a brightness correction that is disclosed. A photo with a circle or arrow may be added as a second copy; the original stays.

### 6.4 Vehicles

Auto physical-damage inspections use the approved appraisal network unless the vehicle is not drivable, in which case a field appraiser or tow-yard inspection is assigned. The appraisal records point of impact, prior damage, odometer, options, and whether the vehicle appears a total loss under the total-loss rule in CLM-004. Tear-down is authorized only when a hidden-damage question cannot be answered otherwise, and the authorization is in the file before the shop opens the vehicle. Parts removed in a tear-down are held until the adjuster releases them.

## 7. Statements

### 7.1 Recorded statements

A recorded statement is taken when fault, permission, occupancy, the timeline, or the cause would otherwise rest on a single unrecorded conversation. It is not taken as a ritual on every file. Before the recording starts, the adjuster identifies everyone on the line, states that the call is being recorded, states the claim number, and asks the speaker to confirm they are willing to give the statement. If the speaker declines, the adjuster continues the investigation with other evidence and notes the refusal. A single refusal is not, by itself, noncooperation.

The statement covers the facts of the event, the property or injury, other insurance, and prior similar losses only as those points relate to this claim. It does not cover unrelated medical history, immigration status, or the person's finances except where the policy condition makes finances relevant, such as a business-interruption claim or a scheduled-property ownership question. Minors are not asked to give recorded statements. A parent may describe what a young child said, and that description is labeled as secondhand.

If a lawyer or public adjuster represents the speaker, the statement is scheduled through the representative. The representative may attend and may object to questions. They may not coach on the recording. If coaching makes the statement useless, the adjuster stops, notes what happened, and raises the next step — usually an EUO or a written question list — with a supervisor.

### 7.2 Examinations under oath

An EUO is requested only when the policy provides for it and one of the following is true: the loss facts are materially inconsistent and ordinary statements have not resolved them; documents that the policy requires have been withheld after a specific request; SIU has concurred that an EUO is the right tool under CLM-007; or Coverage Counsel recommends it on a coverage question that turns on the insured's knowledge. An EUO is not used to wear a claimant down, and it is not requested on a third-party claimant who has no EUO condition in any contract with Northline.

The request is in writing, gives at least ten business days' notice unless the person agrees to an earlier date, states the place or the video method, and describes the documents to bring. The insured may have a lawyer present. Northline does not object to that. The examiner is a person trained to take EUOs or is Coverage Counsel. The assigned adjuster may attend. A transcript is ordered and placed in the file. The insured may obtain a copy under the policy term or the state addendum. After the EUO, the adjuster writes what was learned that the investigation did not already know. An EUO that changes nothing is not repeated.

### 7.3 Third-party claimants and witnesses

Witnesses are contacted once, politely, and told they are not required to speak unless a legal process requires it. Their statement is recorded only with permission. Neighbors are not told the insured's limits, reserve, or medical details. If a witness is hostile or is a minor, the adjuster stops and asks a supervisor before a second attempt. Surveillance of a claimant is not ordered by an adjuster. It is an SIU or defense-counsel tool, approved under CLM-007 or by the litigation supervisor, and it is used only when the injury claim and the observed activity cannot be reconciled any other way. Adjusters do not follow claimants in their own cars.

## 8. Documents and medical information

Requests are specific: "the plumber's invoice for the 12 March visit and any photos the plumber took," not "all documents related to your home." The requester gives a reasonable date and a way to send the file electronically. Personal phones of employees are not the destination for medical records or tax returns.

Medical records and bills are requested with an authorization that names Northline, the claim, and the providers, or by a legal process in litigation. The authorization is not bundled into a general release. Records are used to evaluate the injury claimed. They are not used to look for unrelated conditions to embarrass a claimant. Staff who need to discuss records use the minimum necessary. Mental-health, substance-use, and sexual-health records are requested only when the claimant has put that condition in issue, and the request is approved by a bodily-injury supervisor.

A claimant may use their own treating physician. Northline does not require an IME before it will review those records. An IME is appropriate when the diagnosis, the cause, or the duration conflicts with the mechanism of injury or with the bills in a way the records do not explain, and the conflict is material to the value. The examiner is board-certified in the relevant specialty, has no treating relationship on this claim, and is told to give an opinion, not to advocate. The claimant receives the appointment details, travel reimbursement under the state addendum, and a copy of the report if the law or the negotiation requires it. One IME is the norm. A second requires the bodily-injury manager's approval and a reason the first was insufficient.

## 9. Experts

### 9.1 When to hire

An expert is hired when a technical question is material and beyond the adjuster's training. Cause of a large fire, structural movement, the source of water in a wall, accident reconstruction with serious injury, and the measurement of business interruption beyond a simple payroll ledger are typical. An expert is not hired to bless a decision the adjuster has already announced to the customer. The question is written before the expert is retained: "Did wind create an opening that allowed rain in, or did rain enter through a pre-existing gap at the flashing?" A question that says "confirm the exclusion" is sent back.

### 9.2 Choice, instructions, and the report

Experts come from the approved panel unless a manager approves an off-panel expert because of geography or specialty. The assignment letter states the question, the facts provided, the documents provided, the deadline, and the fee cap. Staff do not withhold a document that cuts against the Company's preferred result. The expert's report must separate facts, methods, and opinions. Draft reports that the adjuster rewrites are not the expert's work and are not allowed. Clarifying questions may be sent in writing and saved to the file.

If the expert's opinion supports coverage, the file follows the opinion unless a second expert is retained for a stated methodological reason, approved by a manager. Shopping for a contrary opinion without that reason is a handling defect. If two experts disagree, the adjuster describes both methods and a manager decides which method is more reliable, or the file is compromised with that disagreement disclosed in the reasoning. The customer is not told "our expert disagrees with yours" without the substance.

### 9.3 Cost

Expert fees are an expense reserve under CLM-004. They are proportionate to the amount and the question. A $6,000 engineering review on a $2,500 disputed item requires supervisor approval before it is ordered. The approval note explains the coverage consequence, not the desire to win.

## 10. Liability investigation

On third-party claims the adjuster gathers the facts needed to evaluate legal responsibility under the law of the place of the accident. Police reports are requested. They are not treated as conclusive. The insured driver and the claimant are both asked for their account. Physical evidence — point of impact, skid marks, vehicle locations, weather, signals — is recorded. Comparative fault is analyzed in percentages the jurisdiction uses, and the percentage is explained. A demand package is compared to that evaluation. It is not answered with a token offer that ignores the documents.

Northline does not admit liability to a third party until the coverage screen supports indemnity and the liability evaluation supports responsibility. "We are sorry this happened" is acceptable. "Our insured was at fault and we will pay whatever you want" is not. Any partial admission is consistent with the later negotiation under CLM-005.

For premises claims, the adjuster obtains the incident report, maintenance records for the relevant period, photos of the condition, and the names of employees who were present. Those records are preserved the day the claim is known. A later "the video already overwritten itself" note is a failure if no hold was sent when the claim arrived.

## 11. Timelines

| Task | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Investigation plan | 2 business days from assignment; same day for severe files |
| First inspection offer | Within 5 business days of assignment, local files |
| Inspection note and photos uploaded | 1 business day after the visit |
| Document request is specific and dated | Sent with or before the first status letter |
| Follow-up on documents the person agreed to send | 5 business days after the promised date |
| Expert assigned, when needed | Within 3 business days after the adjuster decides one is needed |
| Recorded statement, when needed | Scheduled within 5 business days of the decision to take one |
| Status to the customer if the investigation is still open | Every 15 business days, consistent with CLM-001 |

A file is not left in "investigation" with no outstanding request and no scheduled activity. Supervisors run a report of files with no activity for fifteen days and clear the report weekly. If the only delay is a third party who will not respond, the file shows the attempts and the decision that was made with the evidence on hand, or a supervisor-approved decision to wait longer because the missing item is essential and still likely to arrive.

## 12. Integrity of the file

Staff do not alter photos, backdate notes, or delete a note that later looks inconvenient. Corrections are a new note that identifies the error. System timestamps stay as they are. A customer who points out a factual mistake receives a correction if they are right, and an explanation if they are not. Estimates of value and reserve comments are internal. They are still written as if they could be read by a regulator or a court, because sometimes they are. Sarcasm, comments about a person's appearance, nationality, or character, and medical guesses by non-medical staff are prohibited. "Claimant was limping and then walked normally to a car" may be a fair observation if it was actually seen and is relevant. "Claimant is faking" is a conclusion the adjuster may not write. The referral path for that concern is CLM-007.

## 13. Authority and safety

Any employee may collect documents and schedule an inspection. Retaining an off-panel expert, ordering an IME, noticing an EUO, and ordering surveillance require the approvals named in this policy. Adjusters leave a property if they feel unsafe, report the situation to their supervisor, and reschedule with appropriate arrangements. They do not attempt to prove cooperation by staying in an unsafe place. Law enforcement is called for an active threat. The claim is not discussed with the officer beyond what is needed for safety, unless the policy or a legal duty requires a report of a specific crime, in which case the adjuster calls SIU and a supervisor first unless waiting would create immediate danger.

## 14. Quality assurance and training

Quality Review samples inspection notes and investigation plans every month. A file fails if the plan is missing on a severe file, if photos claimed in the note are not in NCF, if a document request is a blanket demand, if an EUO was noticed without the predicate in Section 7.2, or if a represented person was contacted directly. Field ride-alongs occur at least twice a year for staff adjusters in their first two years. Independent adjusters are scored on note quality and photo standards. Repeated failure removes the adjuster from the Northline assignment list until retraining is complete.

Training for new adjusters includes a supervised inspection, a recorded-statement workshop, and a module on medical-authorization limits. No adjuster notices an EUO until they have observed one and completed the EUO workshop. Annual refreshers use examples of spoliation and of expert questions written the wrong way.

## 15. Exceptions

A CAT bulletin may change inspection timing and may allow desk adjustment. It may not waive photo standards, preservation holds, or the ban on secret recording. A life-safety emergency may postpone a full inspection note by one business day while make-safe work is arranged. The note then explains the postponement. There is no exception that allows a denied-access assumption after only one unanswered call.

## 16. Related policies

CLM-001 sets first notice and proof-of-loss timing. CLM-002 uses the facts gathered here and decides prejudice. CLM-004 turns measurements into dollar amounts. CLM-005 does not pay a disputed scope that this policy says was never investigated. CLM-006 is the path for a noncooperation denial. CLM-007 receives fraud referrals and owns surveillance approvals with the litigation supervisor. CLM-009 covers a customer's challenge to an expert or an IME. CLM-010 may change field deployment and does not change evidence integrity.

## Appendix A — Examples

**The pipe that was thrown away.** The plumber replaced a failed angle stop and discarded the part before the adjuster called. The adjuster does not deny the claim for spoliation on that fact alone. The plumber's description, the invoice, the new part, and the water pattern may be enough. The note records what was lost and why the remaining evidence is or is not sufficient. A preservation hold is sent the same day for any remaining wet materials if cause is still open.

**One missed inspection.** The insured misses Tuesday's appointment and calls Wednesday to apologize. The adjuster offers two new times. The file does not say "failure to cooperate." If the insured then refuses three written offers over three weeks without a reason, the supervisor reviews a cooperation analysis under CLM-002.

**Represented insured.** A public adjuster writes that all contact must go through the office. The adjuster schedules the inspection through that office and still expects the insured, or a person with keys and authority, to allow access. The public adjuster may attend. The adjuster does not refuse to inspect because a public adjuster is involved, and does not agree to the public adjuster's scope on site without measuring.

**Expert question done poorly.** An adjuster emails an engineer: "Need you to confirm wear and tear so we can deny." The supervisor cancels that instruction. The replacement question is: "Identify the source of water that stained the ceiling. State whether the discharge was sudden or long-term, and the observations that support that timing." Both emails stay in the file so the correction is visible.

**Medical scope.** A claimant treats for a wrist fracture after a rear-end collision and also mentions a ten-year-old back surgery in passing. The adjuster requests the wrist records and the emergency-room record. The old back-surgery chart is not requested unless the claimant is now claiming a back injury from this accident. The bodily-injury supervisor approves any broader request.

**Video hold.** A grocery store reports a fall the day it happens. The adjuster sends a hold that afternoon identifying the date, the approximate time, and the camera covering the aisle, and asks that the recording be kept for thirty days or until copied. A hold sent three weeks later, after the store's loop has overwritten the footage, does not cure the gap. The file then uses the incident report and witness accounts, and the delay is a handling miss.

## Appendix B — Inspection-note checklist

- [ ] Date, arrival time, and people present are recorded.
- [ ] The note was uploaded within one business day.
- [ ] Observations are separated from conclusions.
- [ ] Measurements and materials needed for the estimate are included.
- [ ] Photos match the note and show overall and close views.
- [ ] Originals are unedited; marked-up copies are labeled.
- [ ] Habitability and any make-safe need are stated.
- [ ] The occupant's on-site account is attributed to them.
- [ ] Unresolved cause questions name the next step, not a guess.
- [ ] A preservation hold went out the same day if evidence was at risk.
- [ ] No comment in the note mocks the customer or states a medical or fraud conclusion.
- [ ] The investigation plan is updated to show what this visit closed and what remains.
