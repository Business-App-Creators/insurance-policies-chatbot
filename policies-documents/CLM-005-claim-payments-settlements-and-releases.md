## 1. Purpose

This policy governs how Northline pays a claim once coverage and measurement support a payment. It covers who may approve a check, who must be named on it, what a release may and may not say, when depreciation is returned, and how liability claims are settled without selling the insured short or paying a demand the evaluation does not support. Payment is the part of the claim the customer can spend. A correct coverage decision that is paid to the wrong person, or held for weeks after the amount is agreed, is still a bad claim.

Speed does not outrank accuracy. A payment that has to be stopped, reversed, or reissued because the payee was wrong costs the customer time and costs the Company a handling defect. The rules below are the ordinary path. They are not a reason to withhold an undisputed amount while a disputed amount is still open.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to indemnity payments and to claim-specific expenses paid through the claim system. Premium refunds, agent commission, and vendor invoices for non-claim work are outside it. Structured settlements and Medicare reporting are in Sections 10 and 11. Catastrophe advance payments follow this policy plus the CAT bulletin under CLM-010. Nothing here authorizes payment of an item the coverage screen has rejected. If the screen and the requested check conflict, the check waits and the screen is corrected or the request is withdrawn.

## 3. Definitions

**Undisputed amount.** The portion of a covered loss that Northline has already measured and does not contest, even if other portions remain open. On a property file it is often the agreed repair items. On a bodily-injury file it may be a documented medical bill the evaluation already accepts.

**Dual payee.** A check payable to more than one person because each has a legal interest, such as the insured and a mortgagee, or the insured and a contractor the insured has asked to be named.

**Release.** A written surrender of claims, used on third-party settlements and on certain first-party disputes. A standard first-party property payment is not conditioned on a general release of all policy rights.

**Holdback.** Recoverable depreciation or a retained amount the policy allows Northline to pay after repairs are complete. It is not a bargaining chip.

**Authority.** The largest indemnity payment, or series of payments on one occurrence, that a role may approve. Authority is personal. It is not borrowed by asking a colleague who is out of the office to "just sign."

## 4. Governing principles

1. Pay the undisputed amount. Do not hold it hostage to the open dispute.
2. Name every person the policy or the law makes a necessary payee. Do not name people for convenience.
3. The check amount matches the settlement letter. Verbal deals are written down the same day and sent to the customer before or with the payment.
4. A release is no broader than the dispute being settled. First-party customers are not asked to release extra-contractual rights or future claims as the price of a sum the policy already owes.
5. Authority is checked against the whole occurrence. Splitting checks to stay under a limit is a violation, even if each check would have been proper at a higher authority.
6. Payment dates are kept. If a promised date will be missed, the customer is told before the date, not after the complaint arrives.
7. Staff do not ask a customer to return a payment that was correctly issued because a later reinspection changed someone's mind, except where the payment was a documented overpayment caused by a factual error or fraud. Ordinary estimate changes on unrepaired items are handled in the next payment, not by clawing back funds the customer received in good faith for items Northline agreed.

## 5. When a payment may be issued

All of the following must be true before an indemnity check is requested:

- the coverage screen supports payment of the items being paid;
- the estimate or evaluation supports the dollar amount, after deductible, depreciation, limits, and comparative fault as applicable;
- the payees have been verified against the policy, the mortgagee clause, any lien the file has recognized, and any representation agreement;
- the approver has authority for the occurrence;
- the reserve is at least equal to the payment plus what is still expected, or it is increased in the same session under CLM-004;
- no legal hold, SIU hold, or bankruptcy hold is open on the file. An SIU hold follows CLM-007 and is not improvised by the adjuster.

An advance for make-safe work authorized under CLM-001 may be issued before the final screen when delay would increase the damage. The payment note states that the advance is subject to the coverage decision and will be credited to any later settlement. If coverage is later denied, recovery of the advance is pursued only when the customer was told, before the work, that repayment might be requested and the governing law allows recovery. It is not pursued against a customer who was told the Company would protect the property and who spent the money on that protection in good faith, unless fraud is established under CLM-007.

## 6. Payees

### 6.1 Named insureds

Payment is made to the named insured unless this section requires someone else to be added. If there are two named insureds, both are named unless one has given a written direction that the other may receive payment alone, and the payment does not affect a mortgagee. A direction signed under pressure, or signed by a person the adjuster has reason to believe does not understand it, is not accepted. The adjuster may require both payees on the check and let them endorse it together.

### 6.2 Mortgagees and loss payees

If the policy's mortgage clause applies and the payment is for real property, the mortgagee is named on any payment at or above the threshold in the current payment bulletin. The current threshold is $10,000 for dwelling or building payments, and any payment of any size if the mortgagee has notified Northline of a default or has requested to be named. Payments only for contents, additional living expense, or a scheduled item that is not part of the building are not made payable to the mortgagee. The adjuster confirms the mortgagee's name and address with the policy image or with a written payoff statement. A check to a misspelled lender is a defect.

When a dual-payee check will be difficult for the insured to negotiate, the adjuster may, with the mortgagee's written consent, pay the contractor directly for completed work, or pay into an escrow the mortgagee accepts. Consent is saved in the file. Verbal consent from a local branch employee who cannot bind the lender is not enough. Until consent arrives, the dual-payee check is the default, and the adjuster explains to the insured why the lender is on the check.

### 6.3 Lienholders on vehicles and other property

A perfected lien on a vehicle is protected according to the state addendum. Total-loss payments name the lienholder and the insured in the manner the addendum requires. The adjuster obtains a payoff figure good through a stated date and does not rely on a figure the customer remembers. Repair payments on a financed vehicle are made to the insured and the shop, or to the insured alone, as the addendum and the repair agreement provide. They are not made to the auto lender unless the lender has a right to the repair check under the contract and the addendum.

### 6.4 Representatives and attorneys

If a lawyer or public adjuster has a valid written authorization to receive proceeds, the check may name the representative and the client as the law and the agreement require. Northline does not pay a representative alone unless the authorization expressly allows it and no mortgagee or lienholder is omitted by doing so. Northline does not calculate a public adjuster's fee and does not increase the loss to cover that fee. The fee, if owed, is a matter between the customer and the representative. Staff do not advise the customer whether the fee is fair. They do not refuse to issue the customer's payment because they disapprove of the hiring.

### 6.5 Minors and estates

Payments to a minor that exceed the small-sum threshold in the state addendum are not made payable to a parent personally. They are paid under the method the addendum allows: a restricted account, a guardianship, or a court order. Below the threshold, payment to the parent as natural guardian is allowed only if the addendum says so, and the file contains the parent's identification and a statement of the relationship. Estate payments require letters of appointment that are still in effect. A surviving spouse is not assumed to be the personal representative.

### 6.6 Vendors

Make-safe vendors and repair shops are paid when the insured has assigned the proceeds in writing or has signed a direction to pay, and the work billed matches the approved estimate. Northline does not pay a vendor for work the inspection does not support because the vendor threatens a lien. The insured is told about the dispute and the approved amount is paid according to the payee rules. Any lien process is referred to Legal when papers are served.

## 7. Partial payments and depreciation

### 7.1 Pay what is agreed

When scope is agreed on part of a loss and genuinely disputed on the rest, the agreed part is paid within the timeline in Section 9. The letter identifies what is paid, what is still open, and what will resolve the open part. The customer is not required to sign a full release to receive the agreed part.

### 7.2 Recoverable depreciation

The actual-cash-value payment is issued without waiting for repairs, once the measurement is done. Recoverable depreciation is paid after the insured submits proof that the covered repair or replacement was completed. Proof is invoices, photos of completed work, or a contractor's completion certificate. The adjuster reviews that proof within ten business days and either pays the holdback or writes what is missing. The holdback is not denied because the customer used a different contractor, chose a reasonably equivalent material, or spent less than the estimate. If the customer spent less, the holdback is limited to the amount actually and reasonably incurred above the actual-cash-value payment, unless the policy and the state addendum require payment of the full replacement cost regardless. The Northline homeowners form pays replacement cost incurred. It does not pay a windfall above the cost incurred. The letter says this in plain language when the holdback is requested.

A time limit in the policy for completing repairs is enforced only after the adjuster has confirmed the limit applies, has warned the insured before it expires, and has considered a reasonable extension for permit delays, contractor shortages, or a catastrophe. Extensions are given in writing. A denial of holdback solely for lateness requires supervisor approval under CLM-006.

## 8. Liability settlements and releases

### 8.1 Evaluation before offer

Every offer is inside an evaluation written under CLM-004. The first offer on a bodily-injury claim is a good-faith offer based on that evaluation, not a token. The Company does not open at a fraction of a known value to "leave room." Negotiation may move within the range. Movement above the range requires a revised evaluation and the authority that matches the new number. A demand above the policy limit is handled with the excess-exposure rules in Section 8.3.

### 8.2 Form of release

A third-party release identifies the parties, the event, the claim number, and the amount. It releases bodily-injury claims or property-damage claims or both, as the payment intends. It does not release claims the payment does not cover. A property-damage settlement does not include a hidden bodily-injury release when the person has treated and those bills are unpaid, unless the person is represented and the release is explicit. Minors' releases follow Section 6.5. A release of a lienholder's interest is obtained when the settlement is meant to clear that lien.

First-party disputed amounts — a fight about price, not about whether any coverage exists — may be resolved with a dispute-specific release that states the items closed and the items left open. A first-party release that waives the appraisal right, the right to recoverable depreciation, or the right to complain to a regulator is not used. Regulatory complaint rights cannot be waived by a release in any event, and staff do not imply that they can.

### 8.3 Limits and excess exposure

When a liability evaluation, a demand, or a suit approaches the policy limit, the examiner notifies the insured in writing of the limit, the demand, and the insured's right to retain personal counsel at the insured's own expense to advise on excess exposure. If a limits demand is made with a reasonable time to respond, and the liability and damages evaluation support paying the limit, the file is referred the day the demand arrives to a claim manager for a limits decision. Letting a limits demand expire without a documented decision is a serious handling failure. Contribution from other policies is pursued under CLM-002 and CLM-008 and does not excuse a missed demand deadline on Northline's own limit.

Defense is not withdrawn because the limit has been tendered unless the policy and the governing law allow withdrawal and Coverage Counsel agrees. Any such step is communicated to the insured before it happens.

### 8.4 No admission as a bargaining tool

Releases may say the payment is not an admission of liability where that is accurate. They are not conditioned on the claimant agreeing to a fact the claimant disputes. Confidentiality clauses are not requested on ordinary claims. They require Legal's approval and a real reason, such as a settlement with a minor celebrity exposure or a commercial dispute the parties have both asked to keep quiet. A confidentiality clause is never used to stop a customer from talking to a regulator.

## 9. Timelines

| Step | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Issue an agreed or undisputed property or auto payment | 5 business days after agreement and payee verification |
| Issue a liability offer once the evaluation is ready and coverage is clear | 10 business days after the records needed for the evaluation arrive |
| Respond to a limits demand | Inside the demand's reasonable deadline, and never later than the deadline without a manager's same-day plan |
| Review depreciation-holdback proof | 10 business days from receipt |
| Explain a short-pay in writing | With the check, not later |
| Stop or reissue a check requested in error | Same business day as the error is found, if the check has not been cashed; Claim Accounting is called immediately if it has |

State addenda that require faster payment override this table. The payment system displays the override. Staff who pay only to the Company table and miss a shorter state deadline are charged with the miss even if the Company table was met.

## 10. Structured settlements and periodic payments

A structured settlement is considered when the claimant is a minor, has a permanent injury, or asks for periodic payments, and the amount is large enough that the structure market can serve it. The claimant is encouraged to consult their own adviser. Northline uses approved annuity issuers. The present value of the structure plus any cash portion equals the agreed settlement. Structures are not used to obscure the amount, and they are not forced on a claimant who wants a lump sum the evaluation supports. Documents are reviewed by Legal before funding.

## 11. Medicare, Medicaid, and other benefit rights

When a bodily-injury settlement involves a person who is or may be a Medicare beneficiary, the examiner follows the Company's Section 111 reporting procedure and resolves known Medicare conditional payments before or at settlement, as that procedure and federal law require. Staff do not promise a claimant that Medicare "does not need to know." Medicaid and ERISA plan liens that have been asserted in writing are addressed in the settlement. A lien that has not been asserted is not invented, and a lien that has been asserted is not ignored. Questions about the validity of a lien go to Legal. The injured person is not pressured to sign a release that leaves them alone with a lien the settlement was supposed to resolve, unless they are represented and the release makes that allocation explicit.

## 12. How payments are delivered

The default is electronic payment to a verified account or a check mailed to the address on the policy or to an address the payee has confirmed in writing. A change of address requested only by telephone is confirmed by a callback to the prior number of record or by a written request. Staff do not reroute a payment to a new account because of a single email, and they do not accept wiring instructions that conflict with the payee rules in Section 6. Suspected diversion is reported as a security incident the same day and the payment is held until the payee is re-verified. That hold is explained to the insured if they are reachable by a known number.

Cash is not paid. Gift cards are not paid. A payment is not handed to an agent to "pass along" unless the payee has authorized that delivery in writing and the agent signs a receipt the same day. The receipt is imaged into NCF.

## 13. Overpayments, stops, and recovery

When Northline pays more than the policy owes because of its own arithmetic error, Claim Accounting is told and the customer is given a clear letter. Repayment is requested. It is not demanded in a tone that suggests fraud. If the customer disputes the overpayment, the file goes to a supervisor. Recovery from a customer who spent an erroneous payment in good faith on the repairs Northline approved is waived unless the amount exceeds $2,500 and a manager approves the recovery attempt. Overpayments caused by misrepresentation are handled under CLM-007 and may be recovered in full.

A stop-payment is requested the day a check is lost, stolen, or issued to the wrong payee. The replacement waits until the stop is confirmed. Two live checks for the same item are a serious defect. The person who issues the second check without a stop confirmation is responsible for the recovery effort together with their supervisor.

## 14. Authority, quality, and training

Approvals are recorded in NCF under the approver's own credentials. Sharing a password so a payment can "go out tonight" is a conduct violation. Out-of-office authority is delegated in the system, not by text message. The delegate uses their own limit or the specific temporary limit that was granted, whichever is lower. A temporary limit is logged and expires on a stated date.

Quality Review samples payments each month for payee errors, releases that were too broad, undisputed amounts that were held, authority splits, and missing settlement letters. Any payment to a wrong mortgagee is a priority fail and is corrected within two business days after discovery. Trends are reported to Claim Accounting and to the Chief Claims Officer.

New adjusters issue no payments above the associate limit until they have completed the payee module, including mortgagee and minor exercises. Annual training includes a wire-diversion scenario and a limits-demand scenario. Failing the limits-demand scenario suspends authority to handle demands above $50,000 until the person retakes it successfully.

## 15. Exceptions

A CAT bulletin may allow a flat advance of a stated amount before inspection. The advance still requires a payee check and a claim number. It is credited to the final payment. There is no exception that allows a release of future claims in exchange for an undisputed payment. There is no exception that allows cash. A same-day emergency housing advance for an uninhabitable dwelling may be approved by a supervisor outside ordinary hours, documented the next business day, and paid to the insured or directly to a lodging provider with the insured's consent.

## 16. Related policies

CLM-001 identifies mortgagees and representatives at intake. CLM-002 and CLM-004 decide what is owed. CLM-006 governs any decision to pay nothing. CLM-007 governs holds and recoveries based on fraud. CLM-008 governs salvage and subrogation credits that are not deductions from a customer's first-party payment unless the policy says so. CLM-009 governs disputes after a payment or an offer. CLM-010 governs catastrophe advances.

## Appendix A — Examples

**Undisputed roof, disputed interior.** Wind damage to a roof is agreed at $16,400 after the deductible. Interior staining is still under investigation because cause is not settled. The roof payment is issued within five business days. The letter says the interior is open. No release is requested. Holding the roof check until the interior engineer reports is a violation unless the engineer question could show the roof itself is not covered, and that possibility is documented.

**Mortgagee spelling.** The declarations say "First Harbor Bank, ISAOA." The adjuster adds the bank on a $22,000 dwelling check using that name and the mailing address from the policy image, confirmed by the bank's payoff letter. A check made only to the insured because the insured said the loan was "almost paid off" is a defect. The payoff letter settles the question.

**Depreciation release.** The insured replaces a roof for $1,100 less than the replacement-cost estimate and sends the invoice and photos. Northline pays recoverable depreciation up to the amount incurred, not the full holdback above the actual cost. The letter shows the math. The adjuster does not refuse the holdback because the shingle brand differed from the estimate when the replacement is of like kind and quality.

**Limits demand.** A claimant's lawyer demands the $100,000 bodily-injury limit within fourteen days and encloses medical records of a surgery. The evaluation supports the limit. The examiner notifies the insured the day the demand arrives and refers the file to a manager that day. The manager's decision is recorded before the fourteenth day. A diary set for the thirteenth day with no evaluation in the file is a handling failure even if someone pays on the last afternoon.

**Release too broad.** An adjuster agrees to pay a disputed fence invoice and sends a release of "all claims, known or unknown, arising out of the policy." The supervisor withdraws that release. The replacement text closes only the fence item for the stated date of loss and states that additional living expense, if any remains, is unaffected.

**Wrong account.** An email from a public adjuster gives new wiring instructions that do not match the direction to pay on file. The adjuster does not wire the funds. The adjuster calls the insured at the number already in the file, confirms the payee, and reports the email as a possible diversion. The payment goes out only after that confirmation, by the method Section 12 allows.

## Appendix B — Payment checklist

- [ ] Coverage screen allows every item in the check.
- [ ] Math for deductible, depreciation, limits, and fault is attached and matches the letter.
- [ ] All required payees are named, spelled as in the policy or payoff letter.
- [ ] No mortgagee is omitted on a building payment at or above $10,000.
- [ ] Any representative's authority is in writing and does not erase a lienholder.
- [ ] Minor and estate payments follow the addendum.
- [ ] The occurrence total is inside the approver's authority.
- [ ] The reserve covers this payment and what remains.
- [ ] No SIU, legal, or bankruptcy hold is open.
- [ ] The release, if any, is no broader than the dispute.
- [ ] The insured was warned of excess exposure if a limits demand is involved.
- [ ] Address or account changes were verified with a known contact.
- [ ] The customer letter identifies what is paid and what is still open.
- [ ] Medicare or asserted liens are addressed on bodily-injury settlements.
