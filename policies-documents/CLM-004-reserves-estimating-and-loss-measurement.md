## 1. Purpose

A reserve is the Company's best current estimate of what it will ultimately pay on a file, plus the expenses it will incur to handle the file. An estimate is the measurement of a property or vehicle repair. They are related and they are not the same. This policy tells adjusters how to set both, how to change them, and how to keep uncovered damage out of an indemnity number. Finance uses the aggregate of case reserves, with its own bulk and incurred-but-not-reported provisions, to state the Company's liabilities. A reserve that is posted high "so nobody asks" and a reserve that is posted low "until we are sure" both distort that statement. Neither is allowed.

Customers are not told the reserve. The reserve is not a settlement offer and it is not an admission. Settlement authority lives in CLM-005 and is set from the evaluation, which should sit inside the reserve, not above it.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to every open indemnity claim and to expense reserves on files that will incur outside cost. Bulk reserves, catastrophe loads, and actuarial IBNR are Finance's responsibility. Adjusters do not post IBNR on a file and do not reduce a case reserve because they heard the accident year is redundant. Reinsurance reporting thresholds are in Section 11. Meeting a reinsurance trigger is not a reason to understate a reserve, and avoiding a trigger is not a reason to overstate one.

Independent adjusters recommend reserves and write estimates. Northline examiners post the reserve. An estimate prepared by a contractor or a public adjuster is evidence of cost. It becomes Northline's measurement only when an adjuster adopts it or reconciles it line by line.

## 3. Definitions

**Case reserve.** The file-level estimate of future indemnity payments and, separately, future loss-adjustment expense, as of today. It includes amounts already recommended but not yet paid. It is reduced when payments are made only in the sense that the remaining reserve equals the new ultimate estimate minus what has been paid. Staff do not "use up" a reserve by paying and forgetting to reset the ultimate.

**Expense reserve (ALAE).** Allocated loss-adjustment expense the file will incur: independent adjusters, engineers, defense counsel, court reporters, IMEs. Unallocated company overhead is not reserved on the file.

**Incurred.** Paid plus remaining case reserve. A reserve change that does not change the ultimate estimate of the loss is a reallocation between paid and outstanding, not a new view of the claim. The note should say which one it is.

**Estimate.** A line-item measurement of the cost to repair or replace damaged covered property, using the approved estimating platform, current local price lists, and the scope the inspection supports.

**Betterment.** A deduction for the difference in value between a new item and the item that was damaged, taken only when the policy or state law allows depreciation or betterment and only on items that are actually improved. It is documented item by item.

**Total loss.** A vehicle or other property whose repair cost plus projected supplements meets or exceeds the total-loss threshold in the state addendum, or whose structural damage makes a safe repair unreasonable even below that threshold.

**Stair-stepping.** A pattern of small reserve increases that track payments already decided, instead of an honest ultimate posted when the facts were known. Stair-stepping is a defect even if the final paid amount is correct.

## 4. Governing principles

1. The reserve matches the current evaluation. If the evaluation moves, the reserve moves in the same work session.
2. Post the ultimate, not the next payment. A roof that will cost $18,000 is reserved at the expected payout, not at the $2,500 emergency advance.
3. Indemnity and expense are reserved separately and coded to the right coverage part.
4. Uncovered damage is not reserved as indemnity. If coverage is in doubt, the reserve reflects the probability-weighted outcome or the conservative probable outcome the Reserve Committee specifies for that line in the current reserve bulletin. The bulletin is followed. Personal caution is not a second method.
5. Depreciation, deductibles, and salvage are applied in the evaluation the way the payment will apply them, so the reserve is a forecast of the check, not of the contractor's gross estimate.
6. Nobody lowers a reserve to hit a financial target. Nobody raises a reserve to justify a settlement they are not otherwise prepared to explain.
7. Reserve notes state facts and numbers. They do not advocate.

## 5. Initial reserves

### 5.1 Intake schedule

At first notice, before the investigation has produced an estimate, the opener posts the intake reserve from the current schedule published by the Reserve Committee. The schedule sets amounts by line, peril, and a small set of severity flags: injury reported, airbag deployment, dwelling uninhabitable, commercial operations interrupted, attorney representation already in hand, and fatality. The schedule is a starting point, not a cap. If the caller describes a loss that is obviously larger — a total commercial building fire, a tractor-trailer fatality — the opener posts the schedule amount and immediately refers the file to a supervisor to set a realistic initial reserve the same day. Leaving a total-loss house fire at the $5,000 intake default overnight is a reserve failure.

### 5.2 First evaluation

The assigned adjuster replaces the intake reserve with an evaluated reserve within ten business days of assignment, or within three business days after the inspection, whichever is earlier. Liability files with no medical information use the injury-pending reserve in the schedule and a diary to obtain bills. A liability file is not left at the intake number for sixty days because "we have no records yet" if a demand, a hospital admission, or a lawyer's description of surgery is already in the file. Those facts support a higher placeholder and a plan to refine it.

A zero indemnity reserve is allowed only when the coverage screen concludes there will be no payment, or when the file is truly report-only and the note explains why no claimant and no injury exist. A zero reserve is not used to park a file the adjuster has not read.

## 6. Property measurement

### 6.1 Scope

The estimate includes only damaged property related to the covered cause, plus operations the policy covers, such as reasonable tear-out, debris removal, and code items the coverage screen has allowed under CLM-002. Pre-existing damage is listed and excluded, with photos. Undamaged property is included only when the matching rule or the policy requires it, and the line cites that rule. The adjuster does not add items to avoid an argument, and does not omit items the photos show because the customer was difficult.

Measurements follow the inspection note. If the estimate and the note disagree, the estimate is wrong until the note is corrected or the estimate is brought back into line. Unit costs come from the approved price list for the ZIP code and the month. Manual overrides are allowed for a documented local condition — a material shortage, a historic material, a necessary specialty trade — and the override reason is on the line. A global increase of every line by a percentage, to "be fair," is not an override. It is an unpriced negotiation and it belongs in settlement authority, not inside the estimating platform.

### 6.2 Overhead, profit, and labor

Overhead and profit are included when the repairs require coordination of three or more trades, or when the state addendum requires them more broadly. They are not included on a single-trade repair that a homeowner or one contractor can do without a general contractor, unless the insured reasonably incurs general-contractor cost and the addendum treats that cost as part of the loss. Sales tax is applied where the work is taxable. Labor minimums and trip charges are used as the platform intends, once per trade per visit, not once per room as a way to increase the total.

### 6.3 Depreciation and holdback

Where the policy pays actual cash value until repair is complete, the adjuster calculates depreciation by item, using age, condition, and useful life. A flat percentage applied to the whole estimate is allowed only for a contents list of many small items of similar age, and the percentage is explained. Recoverable depreciation is released under CLM-005 when the insured documents that the repair or replacement was done. The first payment letter states the actual-cash-value amount, the recoverable amount, and what proof will release it. Nonrecoverable depreciation is identified as such so the customer is not told to send receipts for money the policy will not return.

### 6.4 Contents and business personal property

Contents estimates use the insured's inventory, photos, and, when needed, a sampling method a supervisor approves. The adjuster does not demand receipts for every ordinary household item on a total-contents fire. Proof is proportionate. High-value items, scheduled property, and items the insured did not own are documented more strictly. The adjuster asks where an item was kept and who owns it when the occupancy is shared. Commercial business-personal-property estimates use the insured's records. A forensic accountant is considered under CLM-003 when the records and the claim diverge by more than the examiner's authority or when the interruption claim is based on projected sales rather than history.

### 6.5 Building code and matching dollars

Code items are estimated only after CLM-002 allows them, and only to the code section in the file. Matching lines cite the state rule. If the adjuster believes matching is owed and the supervisor disagrees, Section 11 of CLM-002 on internal disagreement applies. The reserve follows the view that is currently the Company's evaluation, and it is changed the day that view changes. The difference is not left off the reserve because an appeal might fail.

## 7. Vehicle measurement

Repair estimates use the approved network's labor rates and procedures unless a documented deviation is approved. Betterment on tires, batteries, and wear items is taken only under the state addendum. The adjuster audits for unrelated prior damage and for operations that the photos do not support. Supplements are reviewed within three business days. A supplement that changes the repair method — weld versus replace, structural versus cosmetic — is inspected or photo-documented before approval.

Total-loss values use the approved valuation guide for the local market, adjusted for documented condition, mileage, and options. A comparable-vehicle list is saved in the file. Tax, title, and transfer fees are included or excluded exactly as the state addendum requires. The insured is shown the valuation before the total-loss payment is issued, and is given a fair chance to submit comparables the guide missed. Salvage handling follows CLM-008. The reserve on a likely total loss is the expected settlement plus fees, minus expected salvage only if the reserve method in the bulletin says to net salvage. The current bulletin nets expected salvage on auto physical damage and does not net it on property until salvage is certain.

## 8. Liability evaluation

Bodily-injury reserves are built from liability, damages, and coverage, in that order. The note states the fault percentage, the medical specials to date, a reasonable projection of future care if the records support one, wage loss supported by employer confirmation or a reasonable documented estimate, and general damages consistent with the jurisdiction and the injury. The evaluation is a range, and the reserve is the expected outcome inside that range, not the top of the range and not the plaintiff's demand. Demands are recorded. They are not copied into the reserve field.

A surgery recommendation that no surgeon has made is not reserved. A surgery that has been recommended in writing by the treating physician is reserved at a reasonable cost unless a contrary IME, approved under CLM-003, supports a different figure. Future care that is possible but unlikely is described and is not reserved at one hundred percent. The reserve bulletin tells the examiner whether to use expected value or a stated percentile for that uncertainty. Examiners follow the bulletin.

Property-damage liability is reserved at the supported repair or total-loss figure, plus documented rental or loss of use for a reasonable period. Loss of use is not reserved at the claimant's asking rate if that rate exceeds the local market and the addendum allows a market measure. Comparative fault reduces the indemnity reserve on the liability file. It does not reduce a first-party collision payment unless the policy is one of the uncommon forms that makes collision fault-based. The standard Northline collision form is not fault-based.

Defense counsel's liability view is considered and is not a substitute for the examiner's reserve. If counsel values the case differently, both numbers are in the file and the claim manager reconciles them within five business days. Until then the higher number is carried if the difference is an indemnity exposure the Company may have to pay, so the reserve is not light while the debate continues. The reconciliation note explains the final choice.

## 9. Changing the reserve

A reserve is revised the day the evaluation changes: a new medical bill of consequence, a supplement, a coverage decision, a liability percentage change, a demand that reveals previously unknown injury, a denial, or a settlement recommendation. The note states the old ultimate, the new ultimate, and the fact that moved it. Changes of more than $25,000, or more than twenty-five percent of the prior indemnity reserve when the prior reserve exceeds $10,000, require supervisor approval before or the same day they are posted. Changes that cross a reinsurance threshold require the notice in Section 11 and supervisor approval regardless of size.

Closing a file without payment requires the reserve to be taken to zero with a reason that matches the closure code: denial issued, withdrawn, no contact after the attempts CLM-001 requires, or paid in full. Closing a file at a residual reserve to "keep it warm" is not permitted. If more payments are likely, the file stays open.

Reopening follows CLM-001. The reserve is posted again on the day of reopen at the new expected cost, not left at zero until someone remembers.

Reducing a reserve because a payment was made, without reconsidering the ultimate, is the ordinary paid-to-outstanding movement and needs only a one-line note. Reducing a reserve because a manager asked the team to "watch severity" is prohibited. Any such request is reported to the Chief Claims Officer and the Chief Financial Officer. Good-faith revisions after new facts are not that request, and staff are not punished for raising a reserve when the facts require it.

## 10. Authority over estimates and reserves

Payment authority in CLM-005 and reserve authority are aligned but not identical. An adjuster may post a reserve up to the reserve limit for their role even when the payment will later need a higher approver, so the balance sheet is not waiting on the approver's calendar. The current limits are:

| Role | Reserve limit (indemnity ultimate) | Estimate approval |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claim associate | $5,000 | Desk estimates within the bulletin |
| Adjuster | $25,000 | Standard property and auto estimates |
| Senior adjuster | $75,000 | Includes supplements within the limit |
| Claim supervisor | $150,000 | Overrides and matching disputes |
| Claim manager | $500,000 | Large-loss estimates with the large-loss unit |
| Vice President, Claims | Above $500,000 | Any file referred by the large-loss unit |

A person may not split a loss into pieces to stay inside a limit. One occurrence is one authority test, even if it is paid in several checks. Expense reserves have a separate limit at one half of the indemnity limit for the role, and defense-counsel budgets above $25,000 go to the litigation supervisor regardless of that fraction.

## 11. Large loss and reinsurance

Any file whose indemnity reserve reaches $250,000, any fatality, any suit seeking class treatment or extra-contractual damages, and any loss that meets a treaty reporting trigger is reported to the large-loss unit and to Reinsurance the day the threshold is crossed. The large-loss unit reviews the evaluation within five business days. Reinsurance notices are factual. They are not an invitation for the reinsurer to direct the claim. Settlement strategy stays with Northline, subject to any treaty right the Reinsurance department actually confirms in writing. Adjusters do not call reinsurers directly.

A clash notice is made when several files from one event may aggregate. The event number from CLM-001 is the key. CAT aggregations follow CLM-010 as well as this section.

## 12. Quality of the numbers

The Reserve Committee reviews development quarterly: initial reserve versus reserve at ninety days versus ultimate paid, by line and by office. Persistent under-reserving at first evaluation, defined as a median upward development above the Committee's threshold, results in a revised intake schedule or in coaching, not in a quiet instruction to pad. Persistent over-reserving is treated the same way. Individual files are referred to quality review when a reserve moves more than three times in thirty days without a change in facts, when a payment exceeds the reserve that was on the file the day before with no same-day increase, or when depreciation is a flat rate the note cannot explain.

Estimates are audited against photos on a sample each month. Unsupported line items are removed and the estimator is coached. A pattern of unsupported lines removes estimating authority until retraining. Public-adjuster and contractor estimates that were accepted without a reconciliation are an automatic fail if the difference from Northline's figure exceeded $5,000.

## 13. What customers are told

Customers are told the measurement of their covered repair: the scope, the price, the deductible, the depreciation, and the net payment. They are not told "your reserve is $18,000." If a customer asks what the Company "has set aside," the adjuster explains that internal accounting figures are not the settlement and then returns to the estimate. The estimate is shared. Hiding the scope while asking the customer to accept a check is not permitted. Disputes about price are handled by comparing line items, by a second inspection when the dollar gap justifies it, and by the appraisal condition when the policy provides appraisal and CLM-009 says it is time to offer it. They are not handled by silently moving the reserve to the customer's number.

## 14. Training and exceptions

No employee posts a reserve above the intake schedule until they have completed the reserve module and have had five evaluated reserves reviewed. Estimators complete the platform certification for their line. Annual training covers stair-stepping, depreciation, and the difference between a demand and an evaluation, using redacted files from the prior year.

There is no financial-close exception that allows a reserve change to be held until the next month. If the system is unavailable on the last day of a month, the outage log records the evaluations that must be posted first when the system returns, and Finance is told the same day which large files are affected. A CAT bulletin may authorize a simplified intake reserve for the first seventy-two hours. It may not authorize leaving that simplified number in place after the inspection.

## 15. Related policies

CLM-001 requires an intake reserve the day the file opens. CLM-002 decides which dollars are covered before they enter the indemnity estimate. CLM-003 supplies the measurements and the expert opinions the estimate relies on. CLM-005 pays inside the evaluation this policy produced and releases recoverable depreciation. CLM-006 removes indemnity reserves when a denial is issued, leaving expense if work remains. CLM-007 may change an evaluation when SIU finds material misrepresentation. CLM-008 handles salvage credits. CLM-009 handles appraisal and other disputes about the number. CLM-010 sets CAT estimating rules and price-list deviations during a declared event.

## Appendix A — Examples

**Intake then evaluation.** A homeowner reports a kitchen water loss. Intake posts the schedule amount of $7,500. Three days later the inspection supports $11,200 of covered tear-out and repair, a $4,800 pipe item that is not covered, and a $1,000 deductible. Depreciation is $900, of which $700 is recoverable. The indemnity reserve becomes $11,200 minus $1,000 minus $900, or $9,300, plus a note that $700 may return if repairs are completed. The uncovered pipe is not reserved. Posting $16,000 "for the whole estimate" is wrong. Posting $7,500 still, because the check has not been requested, is also wrong.

**Stair-step.** An auto injury file is reserved at $5,000. The adjuster then receives records of a fracture and physical therapy and pays $5,000, raises the reserve to $8,000, pays $3,000 more, and raises it again when a lawyer sends a demand. The facts for a $25,000 evaluation were in the first set of records. The pattern is stair-stepping. The supervisor resets the method and coaches the adjuster. The cure is not to jump the reserve to an unsupported $100,000.

**Do not follow the demand.** Counsel for a claimant demands $450,000. Specials are $22,000, the fracture has healed, and fault is agreed. The examiner's range is $60,000 to $85,000. The reserve is set at $75,000 with the range explained. It is not set at $450,000 to match the letter, and it is not set at $22,000 to match the bills and ignore general damages.

**Total loss arithmetic.** The valuation is $14,200. The state addendum requires sales tax and title fees of $980. Expected salvage is $2,100 and the bulletin says to net salvage on auto. The reserve is $14,200 plus $980 minus $2,100, or $13,080, until salvage is actually received. If salvage later brings $1,600, the ultimate is revised. The customer sees the valuation and the fees. The customer is not shown an internal salvage credit as if it were a charge against them beyond the contract.

**Manager pressure.** A supervisor tells a team to cut open property reserves by ten percent before quarter-end because results look heavy. The team does not comply. The supervisor's instruction is reported. Files are reviewed for genuine over-reserves based on facts, which may be reduced, and the reductions are documented one file at a time.

**Code item not yet allowed.** A contractor includes $6,400 to bring an undamaged electrical panel to current code. The coverage screen has not allowed ordinance coverage. The $6,400 stays off the indemnity reserve and off the payable estimate. It may be listed in the customer explanation as not covered, with the reason. If the screen later allows it, the reserve moves the same day.

## Appendix B — Reserve-note checklist

- [ ] Indemnity and expense are separate.
- [ ] The ultimate, not the next check, is what the number represents.
- [ ] Deductible, depreciation, and excluded scope are visible in the math.
- [ ] The fact that changed the number is named when the reserve moves.
- [ ] The figure is inside the poster's reserve authority, or the approver is identified.
- [ ] Reinsurance and large-loss notices were sent if a threshold was crossed.
- [ ] The customer-facing estimate matches the covered scope inside the reserve.
- [ ] The note contains no joke, no comment on the person's character, and no reference to a financial target.
- [ ] A zero reserve has a closure or coverage reason.
- [ ] Liability reserves state fault, specials, and general damages, and do not copy the demand.
