## 1. Purpose

A denial tells a customer that Northline will not pay a claim or a part of a claim, and it tells them why in language they can answer. A reservation of rights tells an insured that Northline will investigate or defend while specific policy terms remain in question. These are different letters. They protect different interests. Using a denial when the facts are incomplete, or using a reservation as a soft denial that freezes the claim, violates this policy.

This document sets the content, the approvals, and the timing of both letters. The coverage reasoning itself is done under CLM-002. The investigation that supports the facts is done under CLM-003. This policy does not create a new reason to decline. It controls how a reason already found is communicated and who must agree before it is communicated.

## 2. Scope

Every full denial, partial denial, refusal to defend, and reservation of rights on a Northline claim is issued under this policy. A courtesy explanation that a particular item is outside an estimate — for example, an unrelated rot item the adjuster removed from a water-loss scope — is a partial denial if Northline is refusing to pay an item the customer claimed. It is written as one, even if the rest of the claim is being paid. A note in the estimate with no letter is not enough when the customer asked for the item.

Closing a file as withdrawn, because the insured stated in writing that they do not want to pursue payment and no third party is affected, is not a denial. Closing a file as "no contact" is not a denial either. It is a closure under CLM-001 and it may be reopened. Staff do not use a no-contact closure to avoid sending a denial they have already decided.

Third-party claimants are told when Northline will not pay them, for example because the insured is not legally responsible or because no coverage applies to the insured. The letter to a third party does not dump the insured's private coverage analysis. It states the result that affects the third party and the factual basis at a level the third party is entitled to hear. Coverage Counsel reviews the form of that letter when the reason is a coverage defense rather than a liability evaluation.

## 3. Definitions

**Denial.** A written decision that no payment will be made for the claim, or that no defense will be provided.

**Partial denial.** A written decision that pays some of what was claimed and refuses the rest, including a refusal based on a sublimit, an exclusion of part of the damage, or a finding that part of the damage was not caused by the covered event. A deductible is not a partial denial. It is an application of a limit on payment and is explained in the payment letter under CLM-005.

**Reservation of rights.** A written notice that Northline will continue to handle the claim or the defense, and that specified terms may later defeat or limit coverage. It preserves a defense only to the extent the governing law allows. It is not a finding that the insured breached the policy.

**Prejudice finding.** The factual conclusion, required in many states before a condition can support a denial, that the Company was harmed in its investigation or defense. The finding is written under CLM-002 before this policy's letter is approved.

**Reconsideration.** A second look at a denial because the customer supplied new facts, a new legal point, or a complaint. Reconsideration is handled under Section 10 and, if the customer frames it as an appeal, under CLM-009.

## 4. Governing principles

1. The letter is specific enough that the customer can gather the facts or the policy language that would change the result.
2. Every reason the Company is actually relying on is in the letter. Reasons held back for later are treated as waived if the jurisdiction would treat them as waived. Adjusters do not keep a private second theory "in case we need it."
3. Reasons the Company is not relying on are omitted. A letter that quotes five exclusions in the hope that one will stick is a defective letter.
4. The tone is direct and respectful. The customer is not told they were foolish to report the claim, and they are not praised while being denied in a way that obscures the decision.
5. No denial is issued orally. If a customer asks on the phone, the adjuster says the decision will be in writing and when it will be sent.
6. A reservation is sent as soon as the Company has a good-faith question, not after months of unconditional handling that may waive the question.
7. Undisputed covered amounts are paid under CLM-005 even when a partial denial or a reservation addresses other amounts.

## 5. When a denial is permitted

A denial may be approved only when all of the following are in the file:

- the issued policy image, including endorsements;
- a coverage screen under CLM-002 that states the result "not covered" or "partially covered," with facts tied to sources;
- the investigation steps that the screen relies on, completed or explicitly determined to be unnecessary;
- for a condition-based denial, the prejudice analysis the state requires;
- for a fraud-based denial, the SIU conclusion required by CLM-007;
- the draft letter;
- the approval required by Section 8.

If any of those is missing, the file is not ready. The adjuster sends a status letter that says what is still being done. That status letter must not use denial language. "We are unable to provide coverage at this time" is denial language and is not used in a status letter.

A denial is not permitted because the customer was rude, because the loss is frequent, because the premium is small relative to the claim, or because the producer asked Claims to "protect the book." Those facts may be referred to Underwriting for a separate renewal decision after the claim is decided on its merits. They are not coverage facts.

## 6. Content of a denial letter

The letter is on the current template for the state of the loss. Templates are maintained by Compliance. A template older than the current addendum is not used. The letter contains:

- the date, claim number, policy number, and date of loss;
- a plain statement that the claim, or the identified part of it, is denied;
- the facts Northline relies on, written so the customer can recognize their own loss;
- the policy language relied on, quoted accurately, with the form number and edition;
- the reasoning that connects the facts to that language, in short paragraphs rather than a string of citations;
- any portion that is accepted and will be paid, with the payment timing;
- what the customer may send if they believe a fact is wrong, and the address or portal method for sending it;
- the right to an internal appeal under CLM-009, including the deadline;
- any appraisal, mediation, or department-of-insurance information the state addendum requires;
- the name and direct telephone number of a person who can explain the letter.

The letter does not say "based on the information you provided" without saying what that information was. It does not accuse the customer of fraud unless SIU has approved that allegation and Legal has approved the wording. It does not predict litigation. It does not say the customer "failed to read their policy."

Partial-denial letters itemize. An itemized table — claimed item, decision, reason — is required whenever more than one item is refused or whenever the refused amount exceeds $2,500. The table uses the customer's descriptions where possible so they can find the line.

## 7. Reservations of rights

### 7.1 When a reservation is required

A reservation is issued when Northline will take an action that could be treated as an admission of coverage if done in silence, and a specific coverage question remains open. Typical triggers:

- defense of a suit in which some allegations would be covered and some would not;
- a large investigation that will continue past thirty days with a live exclusion question;
- payment of make-safe costs before the screen is finished, where the customer could reasonably think the payment accepted full coverage;
- a late-notice file on which the Company is still investigating and prejudice is not yet known;
- an additional-insured tender the Company will defend while it examines the endorsement's trigger.

A reservation is not issued on every file. A routine water loss that is clearly covered does not need one. Sending a reservation "to be safe" on every claim trains no one and frightens customers. If there is no specific term to cite, there is no reservation.

### 7.2 Content and timing

The reservation identifies each policy term that may apply, the fact that raises the question, and what Northline will do next. It states that the insured may hire their own lawyer at their own expense to advise on coverage, and that Northline's defense counsel, if one is appointed, defends the lawsuit and does not give the insured coverage advice. It asks for information the Company still needs. It is sent within ten business days after the trigger appears, and in all events before or with the first defense action. A reservation sent after the Company has defended for months without one is referred to Coverage Counsel to assess waiver before anyone relies on it.

The insured is not asked to sign the reservation and return it. Acknowledgment is not a condition of handling the claim. If the insured disagrees with the reservation, the disagreement is noted and the claim continues. The disagreement is not treated as a failure to cooperate.

### 7.3 Ending a reservation

When the question is resolved in favor of coverage, the adjuster sends a short letter that withdraws the reservation and states the result. Leaving a reservation in place after the question has been answered is a defect. When the question is resolved against coverage, the reservation is followed by a denial that meets Section 6, or by a partial denial, and defense is ended only if the law and Coverage Counsel allow it. The insured receives the denial before defense counsel withdraws, with enough time for the insured to arrange their own defense as the addendum or the court requires.

## 8. Approval authority

| Decision | Minimum approver |
| --- | --- |
| Partial denial under $10,000, exclusion clear on the face of the form, no counsel-review issue from CLM-002 | Senior adjuster |
| Partial denial of $10,000 or more, or any matching, ordinance, or ensuing-loss partial denial | Claim supervisor |
| Full denial of a first-party claim under $100,000 | Claim supervisor, after reading the screen and the draft letter |
| Full denial of $100,000 or more, any refusal to defend, any fraud denial, any denial based on late notice or noncooperation | Claim manager, with Coverage Counsel's written concurrence |
| Reservation of rights on a suit or on a file reserved at $75,000 or more | Claim supervisor, using the counsel-approved template |
| Reservation that includes a right to recoup defense costs | Coverage Counsel |
| Letter to a third party denying indemnity because of a coverage defense | Coverage Counsel approves the form; the supervisor approves the sending |

The approver reads the screen and the letter. Approval is not "see above" forwarded from a queue. If the approver changes the reasoning, the screen is updated so the file and the letter match. A letter that is stricter than the screen, or a screen that is stricter than the letter, is returned.

Independent adjusters may recommend. They do not sign Northline denial letters and they do not tell the customer "this will be denied" while the recommendation is pending.

## 9. Timing

A denial or reservation is sent as soon as the file is ready under Section 5, and no later than the state-addendum deadline. Where the addendum is silent, the Company standard is fifteen business days after the Company has the information it reasonably needs, and in no event more than forty calendar days from notice without a written explanation to the customer of what is missing and why it matters. That explanation resets the conversation. It does not reset the deadline forever. If the missing item is not essential, the decision is made on the evidence in hand.

Sending a denial early to "stop the clock" before the inspection is complete is prohibited unless the missing inspection cannot change the result — for example, the policy was cancelled months before the loss and the cancellation notice is already proved. The file must say why further facts would not matter.

## 10. Reconsideration

A customer may ask Northline to reconsider by writing to the address in the denial letter or by filing an appeal under CLM-009. New facts are investigated promptly. The original approver does not decide the reconsideration alone if the customer has submitted new documents or has alleged that a fact in the letter is wrong. A second reviewer at equal or higher authority reads the file. The result is a new letter: the denial stands, the denial is modified, or the denial is withdrawn and payment is made. The new letter is sent within fifteen business days after the new information is complete, or the customer is told what is still needed.

Reconsideration is not an argument. The second reviewer may agree with the first decision. The customer is still entitled to see that the new material was addressed. A one-line "we stand by our denial" that ignores a new expert report is a failed reconsideration.

Payment after a reversed denial includes the amounts owed and any interest the state addendum requires, calculated from the date the amount should originally have been paid. The interest calculation is shown. Staff do not describe interest as a goodwill gesture when it is required.

## 11. Extra-contractual exposure

A denial that is late, that omits the real reason, that cites the wrong form, or that ignores documents in the file creates a risk beyond the policy limit. Adjusters flag that risk to a manager when they see it on a file they inherit. They do not hide an earlier bad letter by writing a worse one. The repair is a corrected letter, a reconsideration, and, if a suit or a department complaint is likely, notice to Coverage Counsel. Bad-faith labels are not applied by adjusters to their own Company in customer correspondence. The facts are stated to counsel plainly, including the mistake.

## 12. Quality assurance and training

Every denial above $100,000 and a monthly sample of all other denials are reread by Quality Review against the screen, the policy image, and the letter. A letter fails if it cites language not in the issued form, if it omits an itemized partial denial, if it denies on a condition without the prejudice finding, if a reservation was required and was not sent, or if the tone accuses the customer without an SIU finding. Failed letters are corrected within five business days if the decision itself was wrong, or coached if only the drafting was wrong. A corrected decision is communicated to the customer. Coaching without a correction, when the decision was wrong, is not the remedy.

New senior adjusters complete the denial workshop and have five draft letters rewritten with a supervisor before they may approve a partial denial. Annual training uses one overturned denial from the prior year, with names redacted, so the same miss is not repeated. The workshop includes the difference between a deductible explanation and a partial denial, because that confusion is a repeated source of complaints.

## 13. Exceptions

There is no volume exception that allows a denial to go out without the approver reading it. During a catastrophe, CLM-010 may assign additional approvers. It does not lower the content standard. A denial may be sent by email or portal if the customer has agreed to electronic communication or has filed electronically. A paper copy is also mailed when the state addendum requires mail, or when the electronic message bounces. The file records both attempts.

## 14. Related policies

CLM-001 forbids intake staff from denying claims. CLM-002 is the analysis this letter expresses. CLM-003 is the factual support. CLM-004 requires the indemnity reserve to be removed when a full denial is issued, except for amounts still possible on reconsideration. CLM-005 pays the undisputed portion of a partial denial. CLM-007 supplies the only route to a fraud denial. CLM-009 is the appeal path named in the letter. CLM-010 does not create a summary denial process.

## Appendix A — Examples

**Not ready.** The adjuster believes a roof leak is long-term wear. The only support is a single photo of staining, and the roof has not been inspected because the insured has been out of town. A denial is not approved. A status letter asks for an inspection date. The sentence "we are unable to cover long-term leaks" is removed from the draft.

**Partial denial done correctly.** The screen allows sudden water damage and tear-out, and excludes the failed supply line. The letter pays the tear-out and the resulting damage, denies the pipe, quotes the wear exclusion and the ensuing-loss exception, and attaches a two-line table. The deductible explanation is in the payment paragraph, not in the denial paragraph. The customer can appeal the pipe decision without delaying the check for the tear-out.

**Reservation on a mixed lawsuit.** The complaint alleges both negligent maintenance, which the liability form may cover, and a knowing pollution discharge, which the pollution exclusion may remove. Defense is assigned. A reservation goes out within ten business days identifying the pollution exclusion and the allegations that raise it. The letter does not deny the claim. Defense counsel is told not to advise the insured on the exclusion. When the pollution count is dismissed and the remaining count is covered, the reservation is withdrawn in writing.

**Late notice with prejudice unproved.** Notice was four months late. The property is still there and witnesses are available. A denial based on notice is not approved. If other terms defeat the claim, they are evaluated on their own. The notice issue is recorded as considered and rejected so it does not reappear in the letter as a makeweight.

**Fraud wording.** SIU reports inconsistencies in an inventory. SIU has not concluded that the insured misrepresented a material fact. The denial draft says "you fraudulently inflated your claim." The manager removes that sentence. The letter, if a denial is otherwise proper, is limited to the valuation and coverage points that are proved. A fraud denial waits for CLM-007.

**Reconsideration with a new report.** After a wear denial, the insured sends an engineer’s report that the discharge was sudden and that staining the adjuster called long-term was dye from a repair the same week. A second reviewer, not the original approver alone, orders a reread of the photos and the new report. The result letter addresses the engineer’s points one by one. If the new report is more reliable, the denial is withdrawn and interest is calculated. If it is not, the letter says why the method was less reliable, in terms a non-engineer can follow.

**Third party.** Northline concludes its insured was not negligent in a parking-lot impact, based on the scene photos and both statements. The letter to the third party says the Company will not pay because the investigation shows the insured did not cause the impact, and it summarizes the facts. It does not attach the insured's recorded statement or discuss the insured's limits. If the third party later sues, the file is handled as a suit under CLM-001, not as a reason to pretend the letter was never sent.

## Appendix B — Letter checklist

- [ ] The decision word is accurate: denial, partial denial, or reservation. One letter does not blur them.
- [ ] The policy image matches every quotation, including edition dates.
- [ ] Facts are specific and sourced in the file, even if the letter itself is written in plain sentences.
- [ ] No exclusion is cited that the screen rejected.
- [ ] No extra exclusion is cited "in the alternative."
- [ ] Condition denials include prejudice where the law requires it.
- [ ] Fraud is not alleged unless CLM-007 and Legal approved the words.
- [ ] Partial denials are itemized when more than one item is refused or the amount exceeds $2,500.
- [ ] Undisputed sums are paid, not held.
- [ ] Appeal rights and any required appraisal or department language are included.
- [ ] A direct phone number is on the letter.
- [ ] The approver required by Section 8 has read the screen and the letter.
- [ ] A reservation that is no longer needed has been withdrawn.
- [ ] The indemnity reserve matches the letter the day it is sent.
