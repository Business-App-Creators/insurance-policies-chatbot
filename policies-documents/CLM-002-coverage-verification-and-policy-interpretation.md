## 1. Purpose

This policy tells adjusters how to decide whether a reported event falls inside a Northline policy. The decision has three parts, and they are answered in order: whether a contract was in force, whether the event meets the insuring agreement, and whether an exclusion, condition, or limit reduces or removes payment. Mixing those parts produces letters that quote an exclusion before anyone has checked that the policy was cancelled, and denials that collapse when the customer points out an endorsement that was never read.

Coverage work is not a hunt for a reason to decline. It is a reading of the contract the customer bought, applied to facts that have been gathered under CLM-003, and written down so a second adjuster can see why the result follows. Where the law of the applicable state limits an exclusion, requires prejudice before a condition can defeat coverage, or imposes a duty to defend broader than the duty to indemnify, that law is part of the analysis. Adjusters do not apply a favorite exclusion from another file, and they do not assume every form in the jacket was issued.

This document does not authorize the denial letter itself. When the analysis ends in no coverage or in partial coverage, the letter is issued only under CLM-006. When the analysis is uncertain, the file is handled under a reservation of rights or a request for more facts, not under a guessed denial.

## 2. Scope

Every file with an indemnity reserve, and every report-only liability file that names a potential claimant, receives a written coverage screen. Files opened in error under CLM-001 and closed the same day as non-claims do not. Vendor adjusters may recommend a coverage result. They may not send a denial and they may not tell the customer the loss is covered beyond the authority letter Northline gave them.

Underwriters answer questions about what was sold, what premium was booked, and what form version was issued. They do not direct the claim result on a specific loss. If a coverage dispute reveals that the policy was issued on terms Underwriting did not intend, Claims handles the claim on the policy that was actually issued and refers the form error to Underwriting separately. The customer is not asked to bear a back-office mistake by having the contract rewritten after the loss.

## 3. Definitions

**Applicable law.** The state law that governs interpretation of the policy, determined by the policy's choice-of-law clause if it is enforceable, otherwise by the rules Claims Legal publishes in the state addendum. Adjusters do not pick the state with the friendlier exclusion.

**Condition.** A policy term that requires the insured to do or not do something, such as give notice, cooperate, or protect the property. A condition is not an exclusion. In many states it defeats coverage only if the Company was prejudiced. The file must say whether prejudice is required and how it is shown.

**Endorsement.** A form that changes the jacket. If the endorsement and the jacket conflict, the endorsement controls for the subject it addresses. If two endorsements conflict, the one that more specifically addresses the subject controls. If that test fails, Coverage Counsel decides before any letter goes out.

**Exclusion.** A term that takes specified loss, injury, or damage out of an insuring agreement that would otherwise apply. Exclusions are read narrowly when the governing law says so. They are not stretched because the loss is large.

**Insuring agreement.** The grant of coverage, including who is an insured, what property or injury is covered, and what perils or legal obligations are covered. If the grant is not met, the analysis stops. Staff do not spend pages excluding a loss that was never covered.

**Prejudice.** A concrete harm to Northline's ability to investigate, settle, or defend, caused by the insured's failure. Suspicion is not prejudice. "We would have liked to see the pipe before it was replaced" is prejudice only if the file shows that earlier access would likely have changed the cause or cost, and that the insured's conduct, not Northline's delay, caused the loss of that access.

**Reservation of rights.** A written statement that Northline will investigate or defend while telling the insured which terms may later reduce or remove coverage. It is not a denial. The contents and approvals are in CLM-006.

**Whole policy.** Declarations, jacket, all endorsements listed on the declarations, and schedules. A form that is not listed and was not delivered is not part of the contract just because it sits in the Company form library.

## 4. Governing principles

1. Read the policy that was issued for the date of loss, not the current form and not the renewal that started later.
2. Decide the grant before the exclusion. Decide facts before theory.
3. Ambiguity the Company cannot resolve with the governing-law rules is raised to Coverage Counsel. It is not resolved by choosing the reading that costs less.
4. The customer hears the result in writing, in plain language, with the facts and the policy words that matter. Oral coverage opinions are not the decision.
5. A deductible, a limit, and a coverage denial are different things. Applying a deductible means the loss is covered and the insured shares the first dollars. Calling a deductible a denial misstates the contract.
6. Other insurance, additional insured tenders, and contribution are coverage work. They are not reasons to ignore a Northline insured who has presented a loss.
7. Staff separate "the policy does not cover this" from "we do not have enough facts yet." The second sentence requires a list of what is missing and a date the adjuster will look again.

## 5. Confirming that a contract was in force

### 5.1 Policy lookup

The adjuster retrieves the declarations, forms list, and cancellation history for the date of loss from the policy administration system and saves a copy into NCF. A screen print that shows only the policy number and a status of "active" is not the policy. The saved image must show the edition dates of the forms and the limits, deductibles, mortgagees, and listed drivers or locations that apply to this loss.

If the date of loss falls in a gap between cancellation and reinstatement, the adjuster obtains the notice of cancellation, the proof of mailing, and the reinstatement rules before concluding there is no policy. A reinstatement that the policy or state law makes retroactive puts the policy back in force. A flat cancellation that was processed after the loss because of a billing error does not erase coverage that existed on the date of loss. Those files are referred to a supervisor and to Billing before any no-coverage letter.

### 5.2 Who is an insured

The adjuster lists every person or organization seeking coverage and states why each is or is not an insured. Named insureds, resident relatives, permissive drivers, additional insureds by endorsement, and persons insured by a definition in the jacket are checked against the facts, not against the reporter's assertion. A certificate of insurance that a landlord or general contractor holds is evidence of what the certificate says. It is not, by itself, an endorsement. If the certificate promises additional-insured status and the policy does not contain it, Claims notifies Underwriting the same day. The claim is decided on the policy. Any estoppel or certificate remedy is a Coverage Counsel question, not a front-line assumption either way.

For personal auto, the adjuster records who was driving, whether that person was listed, excluded by a named-driver exclusion, or using the vehicle with permission, and whether the vehicle is a covered auto. A household member who took the car without permission is not automatically uninsured; the permission facts are written down. A named-driver exclusion is applied only if the signed exclusion is in the policy image and the governing law enforces it.

### 5.3 Property and locations

For property policies, the adjuster confirms that the damaged building or item is insured property at a described location, or that an off-premises or newly acquired coverage extension applies. A scheduled item stays on its schedule. A business that moved inventory to a new warehouse before the policy was endorsed is examined under the newly acquired or unnamed-location extension, including its day limit and its sublimit. The adjuster does not deny for "unlisted location" without measuring that extension.

## 6. The insuring agreement

### 6.1 Property perils

On a named-peril form, the insured must show the loss was caused by a listed peril. On an open-peril or special form, the insured must show accidental direct physical loss to covered property, and Northline must show any exclusion it relies on. The file states which kind of form applies. Adjusters do not call a special-form homeowners policy a named-peril policy because the denial feels easier.

Cause of loss is a fact. "Water damage" is not a cause. The note must say where the water came from: a sudden supply-line break, a repeated leak the insured knew about, surface water, a backed-up drain, wind-driven rain through an opening the wind created, or condensation. Those facts point at different grants and different exclusions. If the cause is not known, the coverage screen says "cause undetermined" and lists the inspection, expert, or test that will determine it. An undetermined cause is not an exclusion.

### 6.2 Liability and the duty to defend

On occurrence policies, the adjuster identifies the occurrence, the date, and the claimant's legal theory. On claims-made policies, the adjuster identifies the date the claim was first made, whether it was reported in the policy period or extended reporting period, and whether a prior-knowledge or prior-notice condition applies. Northline's commercial general liability policies in this manual are occurrence forms unless the declarations say otherwise. A claims-made analysis pasted onto an occurrence file is a handling error.

The duty to defend is broader than the duty to pay a judgment when the governing law uses the complaint-allegation rule or a similar rule. If the complaint alleges a theory that would be covered, and also alleges a theory that would not, the defense is provided for the suit, subject to a reservation that identifies the uncovered theory and any right to recoup defense costs if the governing law allows recoupment and the policy or law supports it. Adjusters do not refuse a defense because they believe the insured will ultimately win, or because they believe the facts are different from the complaint, unless Coverage Counsel has written that the jurisdiction allows a facts-outside-the-complaint decision and the file contains those facts.

An additional insured's tender is logged the day it arrives. The adjuster compares the endorsement's trigger — for example, liability arising out of the named insured's ongoing operations, or caused in whole or in part by the named insured — to the allegations. A tender that does not meet the endorsement is declined in a letter under CLM-006. Silence for thirty days is not a decision, and it is a service failure.

### 6.3 Auto, first-party and third-party

Physical-damage coverage is analyzed under the collision and comprehensive grants, including the definition of collision and any custom-parts limit. Liability coverage is analyzed under the grant for bodily injury and property damage the insured becomes legally responsible for. Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage is a first-party contract: the adjuster confirms the tortfeasor's status with the best available evidence, tells the insured what that evidence is, and does not require the insured to sue the tortfeasor before Northline will evaluate the UM claim unless the policy and the state addendum say so. Medical-payments coverage is paid according to its limit and its proof requirements without waiting for a fault decision, unless the form truly conditions it on fault, which the standard Northline personal-auto form does not.

## 7. Exclusions

### 7.1 How an exclusion is applied

Before an exclusion is cited, the file contains the exact exclusion language, the form number and edition, the fact that meets each element of the exclusion, and a note on any exception to the exclusion that might give coverage back. An exception to an exclusion is a grant. It is analyzed, not skipped. The wear-and-tear exclusion with an ensuing-loss exception is the most common example on property files: the worn pipe itself may be excluded while the sudden water damage that followed may be covered. The adjuster prices those parts separately. Denying the entire kitchen because the pipe was old is a misapplication unless the form and the facts truly put all of the damage inside the exclusion.

Anti-concurrent-causation wording is applied only as written and only as the governing law allows. The adjuster describes each cause and the sequence. A hurricane file that has both wind and flood is not labeled "flood" because flood is excluded. Wind damage to covered property remains covered if the form covers wind, even when flood also occurred, unless the anti-concurrent clause and state law take that wind damage out. Where the causes cannot be separated, the adjuster requests a cause-and-origin opinion before denying the separable portion. CAT files follow CLM-010 for vendor panels and still follow this section for the coverage test.

### 7.2 Exclusions that need extra review

The following exclusions are not cited in a denial unless a supervisor, and in the marked cases Coverage Counsel, has agreed in the file:

| Exclusion or issue | Extra review |
| --- | --- |
| Intentional loss or expected injury, including any inference from a criminal charge | Supervisor. Counsel if a criminal case is open or the insured denies intent. |
| Fraud or concealment after the loss | Supervisor and SIU under CLM-007. A fraud belief alone is not an exclusion analysis. |
| Pollution, bacteria, or fungus, when the loss also involves a sudden break or a fire | Supervisor, because exceptions are common and easy to miss. |
| Business activity inside a homeowners policy | Supervisor. Incidental business and permitted occupancies are checked first. |
| Vacancy | Supervisor. The vacancy definition, the number of days, and any exception for a dwelling under renovation are checked against the facts. |
| Earth movement or flood on a policy that may have a buy-back endorsement | Supervisor confirms the buy-back is absent, not merely forgotten. |
| Assault and battery or liquor liability on a commercial policy | Coverage Counsel if the complaint pleads negligence in addition to the excluded act. |
| Punitive damages | Coverage Counsel. Insurability follows the state addendum. |

### 7.3 Ordinance, matching, and tear-out

Building-code or ordinance-or-law coverage is paid only when the endorsement is on the policy and the damaged covered property must be repaired under a code the endorsement reaches. The adjuster obtains the code section from the local official or from the contractor's permit set, not from the contractor's unsupported invoice line. Matching of undamaged property — a full roof slope, a run of siding, a floor that crosses a doorway — is decided under the governing state's matching rule and the policy's pair-and-set or part-of-a-pair language. The file cites the state addendum position. Adjusters do not invent a company-wide "we never match" rule.

Tear-out of a building part to reach a broken pipe is covered or not according to the form. Many Northline homeowners forms cover tear-out and replacement of the part that must be removed to access the break, and exclude the pipe itself if the pipe failed from wear. The estimate is split that way. Collapse coverage, if present, is read on its own definition. Not every ceiling stain is a collapse.

## 8. Conditions

### 8.1 Notice and cooperation

Late notice is measured under CLM-001 for the dates and under this section for the effect. The adjuster writes a prejudice analysis: what evidence was lost, why it cannot be reconstructed, and what decision it would have affected. If the property is still available, the witnesses are still available, and the only complaint is that the report came late, prejudice is not established on those facts alone. The condition is then not used as the basis for denial.

Cooperation failures — a refused inspection, a missed examination under oath, a refusal to produce the lease or the maintenance log — are documented with the requests, the dates, and the customer's stated reason. One missed call is not a failure to cooperate. A denial for noncooperation requires the prejudice showing the state requires, supervisor approval, and a letter that tells the insured what remains possible if they cure, if cure is still meaningful.

### 8.2 Protect the property and no voluntary payments

The insured must take reasonable steps to protect covered property. Northline pays reasonable make-safe costs when the loss is otherwise covered, and it considers those costs even while coverage is open if CLM-001 authorized them to prevent worsening. An insured who completes permanent repairs before inspection has not automatically forfeited the claim. The adjuster uses photos, invoices, remaining debris, and contractor interviews. If those materials are enough to measure the loss, the early repair is not a defense. If they are not, the file describes what cannot be verified. That gap is a proof problem under CLM-004, and it becomes a condition defense only when the policy and the law support it.

An insured who pays a third party or makes a repair contract has not, by that act alone, violated a voluntary-payments condition on a liability policy. The condition is aimed at payments and assumptions of obligation that prejudice the defense. Emergency medical transport and minor property mitigation are not treated as a forfeiture. Any denial that cites voluntary payments goes through Coverage Counsel.

### 8.3 Examination under oath and proof of loss

These tools are requested under CLM-003 and CLM-001, not as a substitute for reading the grant. A late or missing proof of loss is analyzed for prejudice and for waiver. If Northline investigated for months without requesting the proof, a later denial that relies only on the missing form is raised to Coverage Counsel before it is sent, because the Company's own conduct may have waived the condition or estopped the defense.

## 9. Limits, deductibles, and other insurance

### 9.1 Deductibles

The adjuster applies the deductible that matches the coverage part. A wind or hail deductible expressed as a percentage is calculated on the stated insurance amount in the declarations, not on the size of the estimate, unless the form says otherwise. The arithmetic is shown in the file: limit or amount of insurance, percentage, dollar deductible, covered damage, amount payable. Two deductibles are applied to one occurrence only if the policy says two coverage parts each carry their own. Earthquake, hurricane, and named-storm deductibles are not stacked on top of the all-peril deductible unless the form says to stack them. When the wording is unclear, the adjuster applies one deductible, the one the form most clearly triggers, and refers the stacking question to Coverage Counsel if the dollar difference exceeds $10,000.

### 9.2 Limits and sublimits

Payment cannot exceed the limit, but the limit is not a target. Sublimits for jewelry, business property, debris removal, ordinance or law, fungi, and trees are identified before the estimate is agreed. A sublimit is explained in the settlement letter so the customer can see why a $40,000 jewelry schedule did not pay a $40,000 contents list. Additional living expense and loss of rents are limited by the policy's time and dollar terms. The adjuster tracks incurred months against that limit in the file, and warns the insured in writing at the halfway point of a time limit so the end of benefits is not a surprise.

### 9.3 Other insurance and contribution

If another policy may cover the same loss, the adjuster identifies it, tenders or requests the other carrier's position in writing, and continues to handle Northline's own duty. Northline does not tell its insured to "go to the other company first" when the Northline policy is primary. Priority is read from both policies' other-insurance clauses. If both claim to be excess, the adjuster refers the file to Coverage Counsel and, while that question is open, protects the insured's defense and the property. Contribution after Northline pays is Recovery's work under CLM-008. It is not a reason to delay a first-party property payment that Northline owes its own insured.

## 10. The coverage screen

### 10.1 When it is written

A preliminary screen is entered within five business days of assignment for standard files, and within two business days for any file with a defense tender, a fatality, an uninhabitable dwelling, or a reserve at or above $75,000. The screen is updated when new facts arrive. The final screen is completed before the first indemnity payment, before any denial, and before any reservation of rights. A payment issued with no screen is a defect even if the payment was correct.

### 10.2 What it contains

The screen uses the NCF coverage template:

- policy number, form numbers, and edition dates relied on;
- date of loss and, if different, discovery date;
- persons seeking coverage and the basis for each;
- facts relied on, each tied to a source (inspection, photo, witness, pleading, expert);
- insuring-agreement conclusion;
- each exclusion or condition considered and the conclusion, including those considered and rejected, so the file shows the work;
- deductible and limit arithmetic;
- other insurance;
- result: covered, partially covered, not covered, or facts insufficient;
- if facts are insufficient, the specific missing items and the diary date;
- citations to any state-addendum rule that changes the reading.

Template fields left blank are returned by quality review. A screen that says "covered, water" is not a screen.

### 10.3 Talking to the customer while the screen is open

The adjuster may explain which coverages are being reviewed and what documents are still needed. The adjuster does not say "we will pay" until the screen supports payment and authority under CLM-005 exists. The adjuster does not say "this is excluded" until the denial is approved. The sentence to use is: "I am comparing the inspection to the water-damage and wear provisions, and I will write you with the result by [date]." A date that the adjuster cannot meet is not offered. If the date moves, the customer is told before it passes, with the reason.

## 11. Disagreements inside the Company

If the assigned adjuster and the supervisor disagree on the result, the supervisor's view is not silently substituted. Both views are noted, and the claim manager decides. If the amount in dispute exceeds $100,000, or the issue is one of the counsel-review rows in Section 7.2, Coverage Counsel's written advice is obtained first. Counsel's advice is privileged. The factual basis that can be shared with the customer still appears in the letter. Adjusters do not write "our lawyer said no" as the reason given to the insured.

A good-faith reading later reversed by a court or by an appeal under CLM-009 is corrected prospectively on that file, including payment of amounts due with any interest the law requires. It is not treated as a disciplinary event by itself. A reading that ignored an endorsement that was in the file, or that used the wrong form year, is a handling error and is coached.

## 12. Authority, quality, and training

Adjusters may complete a coverage screen that results in payment within their payment authority. Any screen that results in a full or partial denial, a reservation of rights, a punitive-damages position, or a refusal to defend is approved under CLM-006 before it is communicated as the Company's position. Independent adjusters submit a recommended screen. The Northline examiner adopts it, changes it, or sends it back. The examiner's name is the name on the decision.

Quality Review scores coverage screens monthly. A screen fails if it cites an exclusion that is not in the issued form, omits an endorsement that is on the declarations, uses a denial where facts are only incomplete, or shows no prejudice analysis on a condition defense. The target is a fail rate under five percent of sampled screens. Themes from failures are fed back into the quarterly coverage clinic.

New examiners do not issue denial recommendations until they have completed the coverage module and have had ten screens reviewed without a material miss. Annual training uses real, redacted files, including at least one ensuing-loss file, one additional-insured tender, and one late-notice file in which prejudice was not found. Passing the clinic is a condition of keeping denial-recommendation authority.

## 13. Exceptions

There is no exception to saving the issued policy image in the file. There is no exception to writing the screen before a denial. A catastrophe volume exception may extend the five-day preliminary screen to the time stated in the CAT bulletin, and it may not extend the screen past the point of payment or denial. If NCF is down, the screen is written on the outage template and pasted into NCF the day the system returns.

## 14. Related policies

CLM-001 opens the file and records the dates this policy uses for notice. CLM-003 supplies the facts the screen is allowed to rely on. CLM-004 prices a covered loss and keeps uncovered items out of the indemnity reserve. CLM-005 pays only what the screen has allowed. CLM-006 sends the denial or reservation. CLM-007 owns fraud and concealment positions. CLM-008 owns contribution and subrogation after payment. CLM-009 owns disagreements the customer raises after the letter. CLM-010 owns catastrophe operations and does not rewrite this coverage test.

## Appendix A — Worked screens

**Sudden pipe break.** A homeowners special form. The supply line under the kitchen sink splits overnight. The insured shut the water off in the morning and reported the same day. The pipe is twelve years old. The form excludes wear and tear and the pipe itself, and it covers ensuing sudden water damage and tear-out to reach the break. Screen result: partially covered. The estimate removes the pipe cost, pays tear-out and resulting water damage, and applies one all-peril deductible. The letter itemizes the split. It does not call the tear-out a goodwill payment.

**Long-term leak.** Same form. The insured noticed staining six months ago, placed a bucket under the sink, and called after the cabinet floor failed. Photos show rot and repeated staining. Result: the sudden-discharge grant is not met for the long-term rot. Any sudden final break is separated if the expert can separate it. The screen does not use the word "neglect" unless the neglect exclusion is in the form and its elements are met. Supervisor review is required because the fungi and wear exclusions both appear in the estimate.

**Late hail, no prejudice.** The insured reports hail eight months after the storm, when selling the house. Invoices and photos still show bruising consistent with the storm date, and the roof is available to inspect. Notice was late. Prejudice is not shown. The screen proceeds to cause and to the hail deductible. The late-notice condition is considered and rejected in the screen so the next reader does not reopen it casually.

**Additional insured tender.** A general contractor tenders a suit by a subcontractor's employee. The additional-insured endorsement covers injury caused in whole or in part by the named insured's acts in the performance of ongoing operations. The complaint alleges only that the general contractor failed to control its own site, and the facts available show the named insured was not on site that week. The examiner refers the defense question to Coverage Counsel because the jurisdiction's complaint-allegation rule may still require a defense. No denial is sent by the examiner alone.

**Certificate with no endorsement.** A landlord produces a certificate stating it is an additional insured. The policy image has no such endorsement and the application did not request one. The screen says the landlord is not an additional insured on the contract. Underwriting is notified. Coverage Counsel is asked whether the certificate plus the agent's email creates an estoppel issue before a denial is sent to the landlord. The named insured's own coverage is analyzed separately and is not held up by the landlord's tender.

**Two deductibles.** Wind damages the dwelling and hail dents the detached garage on a policy with a two-percent wind-hail deductible on the dwelling and a flat deductible on other structures. The adjuster computes each deductible as the form states, shows the math, and does not also take the all-peril deductible. If the form is ambiguous about whether the percentage applies to the garage, one deductible — the reading more consistent with the state addendum — is applied and the dollar difference is noted for counsel if it exceeds $10,000.

## Appendix B — Coverage screen checklist

- [ ] Policy image for the date of loss is saved in NCF, including endorsements and cancellation history.
- [ ] The form year in the screen matches the image, not the current jacket.
- [ ] Every person seeking coverage is accepted or rejected with a reason.
- [ ] Cause of loss is specific, sourced, and dated.
- [ ] The insuring agreement is answered before any exclusion.
- [ ] Each exclusion relied on is quoted, and any exception is answered.
- [ ] Exclusions that were considered and rejected are listed.
- [ ] Conditions include a prejudice paragraph where the law requires one.
- [ ] Deductible math is shown.
- [ ] Sublimits are identified before settlement.
- [ ] Other insurance is either ruled out or tendered.
- [ ] The result matches what the customer will be told.
- [ ] Denial or reservation, if any, is routed to CLM-006 and is not already promised on the phone.
- [ ] Counsel-review items are actually with counsel.
- [ ] A diary exists if the result is "facts insufficient," with the missing items named.
