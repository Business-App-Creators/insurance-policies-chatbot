## 1. Purpose

This policy states how a loss, occurrence, accident, or other event becomes a Northline Assurance claim. It tells staff what information must be captured at first notice, who is allowed to report, how fast the Company must acknowledge the report, and what happens when notice is late, incomplete, duplicated, or delivered by someone other than the named insured.

A claim file is the official record of the Company's handling. The first notice of loss is the moment that record begins. Errors at intake — a wrong date of loss, a missed mortgagee, an unlogged attorney letter, a second file opened for the same event — travel through coverage, reserves, and payment. This policy exists so those errors are caught at the door.

Where a statute, regulation, or the insurance contract sets a stricter rule than this document, the stricter rule controls. State addenda issued by Compliance override the timelines in Section 8 for the states they name. This document is an operating policy for Northline staff. It is not a coverage grant and it does not amend any policy issued to a customer.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 Who must follow it

Every person who can open, receive, or redirect a claim must follow this policy. That includes the 24-hour First Notice Desk, branch claim associates, staff field adjusters, independent adjusters appointed by Northline, third-party administrators handling Northline paper, and any underwriter or agent who is handed a loss notice by a customer. Agents may receive notice. They may not decide coverage, set a reserve, or tell a customer a claim will be paid.

### 2.2 What events must be submitted

A person must submit a claim, or the Company must open a file on its own initiative, when any of the following is true:

- An insured, additional insured, loss payee, mortgagee, or other person claiming under a Northline policy reports bodily injury, property damage, loss of use, or a circumstance that may later produce either.
- A third party asserts that a Northline insured caused injury or damage, even if the insured has not yet called.
- The Company receives a summons, complaint, attorney representation letter, department-of-insurance inquiry, or medical-provider lien that identifies a Northline policy or insured.
- A Northline policyholder reports a weather event, theft, fire, water loss, collision, or other incident and asks whether it is covered. Asking "is this covered?" is notice. Staff open a file and then answer the coverage question under CLM-002. They do not answer coverage on the phone with no file.
- Reinsurance, a fronting partner, or a managing general agent forwards a loss that attaches to a Northline contract.

Staff do not open a claim file for a pure billing dispute, a request for a policy change, or a coverage question about a hypothetical future event. Those go to service or underwriting. If the caller then describes an event that has already happened, the call becomes a claim and the file is opened before the call ends.

### 2.3 Lines of business

The intake rules in this policy are the same across lines. What changes is the data that must be collected and the desk the file is routed to. Personal auto files route to the Auto Liability or Auto Physical Damage queue based on whether injury or only vehicle damage is reported. Homeowners and commercial property route to Property. Commercial general liability routes to Casualty. A single event that touches more than one policy — a house fire that also destroys a scheduled boat and injures a guest — produces one event record and as many claim files as there are policies, linked by the event number. Staff never force two policies into one claim number.

## 3. Definitions

**Acknowledgment.** A written or electronic confirmation that Northline has received the notice, has opened a claim, and has given the claimant the claim number, the name of a contact, and a description of what the Company still needs.

**Claimant.** Any person or organization seeking payment under a Northline policy or alleging injury or damage caused by a Northline insured. The insured and a third party can both be claimants on the same event.

**Date of loss.** The date the injury, damage, or loss occurred. For a continuing condition, such as a slow leak, the date of loss is the date the insured first knew or, with reasonable attention, should have known of the damage, unless the policy defines the date differently. Staff record both the date damage began and the date it was discovered when those dates differ, and they do not pick one silently.

**Event number.** The identifier for the real-world incident. One event may have several claim numbers.

**First notice of loss (FNOL).** The earliest report to Northline, or to an agent the policy designates as a person to whom notice may be given, that a loss or occurrence has happened.

**Late notice.** Notice received after the time required by the policy or, where the policy is silent, after the time a reasonably diligent person would have reported given the facts. Late notice is a coverage issue under CLM-002. It is not a reason to refuse to open a file.

**Named insured.** The person or organization listed on the declarations. Additional insureds, loss payees, and mortgagees have their own reporting rights as described in Section 5.

**Northline Claim File (NCF).** The system of record. A note in email, a text message, or an adjuster's notebook is not the claim file until it is entered in NCF.

**Proof of loss.** The signed statement of the amount and cause of loss that some property policies require as a condition. A first notice is not a proof of loss. Staff must not treat the intake conversation as if the insured had already sworn to the amount claimed.

**Reporter.** The person who actually contacts Northline. The reporter may or may not be the claimant.

## 4. Governing principles

1. **Open the file first.** If the report describes an event that may involve a Northline policy, staff open the claim before they debate whether it will be paid. A refusal to open a file is not a coverage decision, and it leaves the Company with no record of what it was told.
2. **The customer is told the truth about what has and has not been decided.** Intake staff may say "we have opened a claim and an adjuster will review coverage." They may not say "you are covered," "this will be denied," or "we always pay this."
3. **Notice to an appointed agent is notice to the Company** when the policy or agency agreement says so. The clock for acknowledgment runs from the day the agent received the notice, not from the day the agent remembered to forward it. Agents must forward notice the same business day.
4. **One event, linked files.** Duplicate claim numbers for the same policy and the same event are a defect. Staff search before they create.
5. **The person who reports does not have to use any particular words.** "I think my pipe burst," "someone hit my car and left," and "my tenant is being sued" are all notice if they identify an event and a possible Northline policy.
6. **Vulnerable claimants are not processed on the standard script alone.** A report involving a fatality, a hospitalized minor, a person who does not speak the language of the call, or a person who says they cannot safely return to the property is flagged at intake and assigned the same day. See Section 6.4.
7. **Nothing in this policy limits a duty the Company owes under unfair-claims-settlement law.** When this manual and that law diverge, staff follow the law and record the variance.

## 5. Who may submit a claim

### 5.1 Insureds and people acting for them

The named insured, a spouse or resident relative who is an insured under the policy, an officer or authorized employee of a commercial named insured, and a person holding a written power of attorney may submit a claim and may receive claim information. Staff verify identity before they discuss policy limits, prior claims, or payment status. Verification is the caller's full name, the policy number or insured address, and one additional item that is not public: the last four digits of the tax identifier on the billing account, a vehicle identification number, or a recent billing amount. Staff do not ask for a full Social Security number on an unsecured phone call.

A household member who is not an insured may report an event. Staff open the file. They do not release coverage details or payment information to that person until an insured authorizes it in writing or the policy gives that person rights of their own.

### 5.2 Additional insureds, mortgagees, and loss payees

An additional insured may report a claim seeking defense or indemnity for a suit against them. A mortgagee or loss payee may report damage to property in which they have a recorded interest, even if the named insured has not called and even if the named insured disagrees. When a mortgagee reports and the named insured does not, staff still notify the named insured in writing within two business days that a claim has been opened on the property. Payment rules for dual payees are in CLM-005. Intake must capture the mortgagee name, loan number if known, and mailing address so the loss payee is not discovered for the first time at payment.

### 5.3 Third parties

A person who says a Northline insured injured them or damaged their property may report the claim. Staff open a liability file. They do not give the third party the insured's home telephone number, policy limit, or a coverage opinion at intake. They do give the claim number, a description of the next step, and a request for the third party's own damage information. If the third party is represented by a lawyer, all later contact goes to the lawyer once representation is confirmed. See Section 6.3.

### 5.4 Agents and brokers

Captive agents and independent agents appointed by Northline must accept notice and must enter it in NCF, or must send the completed NAC-CLM-100 to the First Notice Desk, on the business day they receive it. An agent who tells a customer "I'll let claims know" has received notice for the Company. An agent may help the customer describe the loss. An agent may not estimate the settlement, advise the customer to wait and see if the damage gets worse before reporting, or discourage a report because the customer's premium might rise. Any such statement is reported to Agency Management the same day.

### 5.5 Representatives, public adjusters, and attorneys

A lawyer, public adjuster, or other representative may submit a claim only with an authorization signed by the person they represent, or with a letter of representation that identifies the client and the event. Form NAC-CLM-108 is preferred. A representation letter on firm letterhead is acceptable if it names the client, the date of loss, and the property or injury. Until that writing is in the file, staff may take the factual report from the representative so the file can be opened, and they may not discuss settlement authority or issue payment to the representative.

Northline does not require a customer to hire a public adjuster or a lawyer, and staff do not suggest one. Northline also does not refuse to speak with a properly authorized representative. Once authorization is on file, copies of claims correspondence go to the representative and to the customer unless the customer has directed in writing that correspondence go only to the representative.

### 5.6 Minors, estates, and incapacitated persons

A parent or legal guardian may report a claim for a minor child. Payment to a minor is governed by CLM-005 and often requires a court-approved guardian or a restricted account. Intake records the minor's age and the reporter's relationship. Staff do not ask a minor to give a recorded statement at intake.

If the named insured has died, the person reporting must identify themselves and their relationship. Staff open the file and request letters of appointment for the estate before any payment or any recorded statement about the merits. Emergency board-up and make-safe work may still be authorized so the property does not deteriorate. That authorization is not an admission that the estate's claim will be paid.

## 6. Channels and required information

### 6.1 Accepted channels

Northline accepts first notice by:

- the 24-hour telephone desk, including the language line;
- the online claim portal and the Northline mobile application;
- email to the published claims address, which is monitored on business days;
- the appointed agent, as provided in Section 5.4;
- postal mail and overnight courier;
- a lawsuit or attorney letter received by any Northline office, Legal, or the registered agent.

Social-media messages, text messages to an employee's personal phone, and voicemail left on a closed branch line are not monitored claim channels. If an employee happens to receive one and it describes a loss, the employee forwards it to the First Notice Desk the same day and does not handle it from a personal device. The Company does not promise a response time on unmonitored channels, and marketing does not publish them as claim contacts.

### 6.2 Minimum data set

A notice is complete enough to open when staff have, or have documented why they do not yet have, the following:

| Item | Why it is required |
| --- | --- |
| Reporter name, phone, email, and relationship to the insured | So the Company can call back and know whom it may speak with |
| Named insured and policy number, or enough to search: name plus address, vehicle, or business | So the file attaches to a contract |
| Date and time of loss, and the date the insured discovered it if different | Coverage, deductibles, and late-notice analysis all depend on it |
| Location of the loss | Jurisdiction, catastrophe coding, and inspection assignment |
| Short description of what happened and what is damaged or who is hurt | Routing, reserve, and injury flag |
| Whether police, fire, or medical care was involved, and any report number | Liability and fraud indicators |
| For property: whether the property is habitable and whether a mortgage exists | Emergency living expense and loss-payee notice |
| For auto: year, make, model, plate or VIN, other driver if known, and whether anyone was injured or taken from the scene | Coverage part and injury desk |
| For liability: name of the claimant if known, and whether a lawsuit or lawyer letter has arrived | Litigation hold and counsel assignment |
| Preferred language | So later contact is not conducted in a language the person does not understand |

If the caller cannot supply a policy number, staff search NCF and the policy administration system by name, address, and vehicle. If no policy is found, staff still record the report as an unmatched notice, give the reporter a reference number, and tell them the search result. Unmatched notices are worked by the desk supervisor within one business day. They are not deleted because the first search failed.

Staff do not require, at intake, a contractor estimate, a recorded statement, medical records, or a signed proof of loss. Asking for those items is part of investigation under CLM-003. Requiring them before a claim number exists delays acknowledgment and is not permitted.

### 6.3 Lawsuits and representation letters

Any employee who receives a summons, complaint, or letter of representation delivers it to the Litigation Intake queue the day it is received, by scan if the original is paper. The original paper is sent the same day by internal courier or overnight mail. Litigation Intake opens or links the claim, places a litigation hold on the file so routine destruction stops, and refers the suit to assigned defense counsel under the panel rules. A lawsuit is not held at a branch for the producer to "look into it." The time to answer a complaint runs from service, not from the day Claims happens to see the paper.

### 6.4 Immediate-harm reports

The First Notice Desk flags and warm-transfers, or assigns within two hours, any report that includes:

- a death, or an injury described as life-threatening;
- a child under 18 taken to a hospital;
- a dwelling that is not safe to occupy, including no heat in freezing weather, an open roof, fire damage, or a sewage backup that has made the home unusable;
- a commercial insured whose operations are stopped;
- a caller who says they are in danger, or who describes a loss caused by a person who is still at the property;
- any report the call-taker believes involves a vulnerable adult who may not be able to protect their own interest.

Make-safe authority for temporary repairs, board-up, and water mitigation may be given at intake up to the associate limit in CLM-004 so the loss does not get worse. That spend is an expense or an indemnity payment according to the coverage part, and it is coded as incurred before the coverage decision is final when waiting would increase the damage. The file note must say that the spend was to protect the property and is subject to the later coverage decision.

## 7. Opening the file

### 7.1 Search before create

Before creating a claim, staff search NCF for the policy number and the date of loss, plus or minus three days, and for the location address. If a file exists for the same policy and the same event, the new information is added to that file. A second file is opened only when a second policy, a second claimant who needs a separate liability evaluation, or a second coverage part that the system cannot hold on the first file requires it. The files are cross-linked the same day.

A weather event that affects many insureds is not a reason to skip the search. Catastrophe coding under CLM-010 is applied after the duplicate check, not instead of it.

### 7.2 What the opener records

The person who opens the file enters the minimum data set, the channel, the date and time notice reached Northline or its agent, and a factual narrative in the reporter's words. The narrative is not rewritten into coverage language. "Caller states the kitchen ceiling fell in after two days of heavy rain and they first saw staining last night" is acceptable. "Covered windstorm; pay the roof" is not. Opinions about fault, fraud, or coverage may be entered only in the designated analysis fields, separated from the factual narrative, and only by someone whose job includes that analysis.

The opener sets the initial line of business, the injury flag if any person may be hurt, the catastrophe code if Claims Management has opened a CAT event, and the diary for first contact. The opener does not set a coverage indicator to "covered."

### 7.3 Initial reserve

Every open indemnity file carries a reserve before the end of the business day it is opened, using the intake reserve schedule published by Finance and Claims. A file with a zero indemnity reserve overnight is an exception that requires a supervisor note explaining why no payment is possible, for example a report-only notice on a liability policy where no claimant has been identified and no injury is alleged. Expense reserves are posted when the file is assigned to a field adjuster or an expert. Reserve method is governed by CLM-004. Intake staff do not guess a high number "to be safe" and they do not leave the reserve at one dollar.

### 7.4 Assignment

Standard files are assigned by the routing rules in NCF: geography, line of business, license where a state requires a licensed adjuster, current workload, and language. A file is not assigned to the employee who sold the policy. Files in which the claimant is a Northline employee, an agent's household, or a relative of the assigned adjuster are reassigned by a supervisor to a person with no personal connection, and the relationship is noted.

The assigned adjuster owns the file until it is transferred or closed. Ownership includes first contact, the coverage screen, the reserve, and the customer updates required in Section 8. A transfer requires a note that says what has been done, what is owed next, and the date of the last customer contact. Silent transfers are a handling defect.

## 8. Service timelines

Times in this section are Company standards. If the state unfair-claims law or the policy wording requires a faster acknowledgment, a faster decision, or a specific content in the letter, the state addendum controls. The First Notice Desk keeps the addendum dates in NCF so staff do not rely on memory.

| Step | Standard | Clock starts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open the claim and give a claim number by phone or portal | During the contact, if the report is by phone or portal | When the report is received |
| Acknowledge in writing (letter or the customer's chosen electronic method) | 1 business day from receipt of notice; same day if the state addendum says so | When notice reached the Company or its appointed agent |
| First outbound contact by the assigned adjuster | 1 business day after assignment | Assignment |
| If no one answers, second and third attempts on different days and at different times, plus a written attempt | Within 5 business days of assignment | Assignment |
| Tell the claimant what documents are still needed, in writing | With the acknowledgment, and again when the list changes | Receipt of notice |
| Status update if the file remains open and no other letter has gone out | Every 15 business days | Last substantive communication |
| Request a signed proof of loss, if the policy requires one and the facts justify the request | Not at intake by default; only after the adjuster has enough facts to tell the insured what to complete | Adjuster's documented decision |

A voicemail that does not say who called, the claim number, and a direct return number does not count as an attempt. A letter sent to an address the file shows is vacant does not count as notice to the insured. Staff use the last confirmed contact and record returned mail the day it comes back.

### 8.1 Content of the acknowledgment

Form NAC-CLM-101, or the portal message that mirrors it, includes:

- the claim number and date of loss;
- the policy number and a plain description of the coverage part under review, without stating that coverage has been accepted;
- the name, telephone number, and email of the assigned adjuster, or of the desk if assignment is not complete;
- a list of information still needed, written as specific items ("photos of the ceiling and the failed supply line") rather than "send all relevant documents";
- a statement that the insured may submit information at any time and that Northline will consider it;
- the fraud warning required by the state where the loss occurred or the policy was issued, as specified in the state addendum;
- for property losses, a short note that temporary reasonable repairs to protect the property may be made, that receipts should be kept, and that permanent repairs should wait for inspection unless delay would increase the damage.

The acknowledgment does not quote a settlement amount, does not waive any policy condition, and does not use the phrase "this claim is not covered" unless a denial has been approved under CLM-006.

### 8.2 Late notice

Staff accept the report whenever it arrives. They record when the event happened, when the insured learned of it, when notice reached the Company, and the reason given for any delay. They do not lecture the caller and they do not refuse the claim number. Whether late notice defeats coverage is decided under CLM-002 after the adjuster looks at prejudice to the Company's investigation. A file note that says only "late, deny" is not an analysis and will be returned by quality review.

Examples that still require a file and a real analysis:

- A homeowner reports hail six months after a storm because they noticed leaks only when they sold the house.
- A business reports a slip and fall four months after the date on a lawyer's letter, and says it never received an earlier complaint.
- An insured rear-ended another car, exchanged information, and called three weeks later after the other driver asked for payment.

In each case the file is opened the day of the call. The coverage question is sequential, not a reason to turn the caller away.

### 8.3 Incomplete notice

If the report lacks a piece of the minimum data set, staff open the file with what they have, send the acknowledgment describing the gap, and diary a follow-up in three business days. They do not hold the notice in an email inbox until the customer sends everything. An inbox is not a claim file. Supervisors review any notice email older than one business day that has not been converted to a claim or to a documented non-claim.

## 9. Proof of loss and other formal statements

Some property policies require a signed and sworn proof of loss within a stated number of days after the Company requests it. Northline requests a proof of loss when the amount claimed is disputed, when the insured is represented by a public adjuster and the figures conflict with the adjuster's measurement, when fraud indicators are present and SIU has concurred, or when the policy condition will be relied on. Northline does not send a proof of loss form on every kitchen-sink leak as a ritual.

When a proof is requested, the letter states the contractual deadline, encloses or links form NAC-CLM-104, and identifies the documents that will help the insured complete it. The Company may extend the deadline in writing. An adjuster who needs more time to inspect must not use the proof deadline as a substitute for scheduling the inspection. If the insured submits a timely proof that is incomplete, staff say what is missing within ten business days and allow a reasonable time to cure. A denial based solely on the proof-of-loss condition requires supervisor approval under CLM-006 and a file that shows prejudice or an express policy failure that the jurisdiction enforces.

Examinations under oath, medical examinations, and recorded statements are investigation tools under CLM-003. They are not part of first notice and they are not demanded in the acknowledgment letter by default.

## 10. Duplicate, reopened, and supplemental claims

### 10.1 Duplicates

A duplicate is a second claim number for the same policy, same event, and same coverage part. The surviving file is the one with the earlier notice date unless payments have already been issued on the later file, in which case the file that holds the payments survives and the other file's documents are moved to it. The discarded number is closed with a cross-reference, not deleted, so the old number still resolves in search. Payments are never "moved" by reissuing them to a new number without a reconciliation note from Claim Accounting.

### 10.2 Reopen

A closed file is reopened when new damage from the same event is reported, a payment bounces, a lawsuit is served, or Recovery finds a subrogation opportunity that was not pursued. The adjuster states why the file was reopened and resets the reserve. A reopen within thirty days of closure is reviewed by a supervisor to see whether the closure was premature. Reopen for a new event, even at the same address, is a new claim.

### 10.3 Supplemental claims

A supplemental request on an open or recently closed property file — a contractor finds additional wet drywall after demolition — is handled on the original claim if it is the same cause of loss and the same occurrence. Staff inspect or document why a second inspection is unnecessary, update the estimate, and do not require the insured to "start over" with a new proof package if the original package plus the supplement is enough to decide.

## 11. What intake staff must not do

- They must not deny a claim, in whole or in part, on the phone. Denials follow CLM-006.
- They must not promise a payment date or an amount.
- They must not tell an insured to repair everything and "send the bills" on a loss that has not been inspected, except for reasonable make-safe work described in Section 6.4.
- They must not discourage a report, suggest that a small claim be paid out of pocket to "protect the policy," or advise a customer on how the claim might affect renewal. Underwriting decides renewal. Claims decides the loss.
- They must not give legal advice. They may read a policy condition aloud and say that coverage has not yet been determined.
- They must not discuss one claimant's medical condition or settlement with another claimant.
- They must not alter a recorded fact because a later speaker prefers a different date of loss. Both statements stay in the file, attributed to the speaker, with the time they were made.

## 12. Communication standards

Staff use plain language. They say "we have not finished the coverage review" rather than "liability is undetermined pending completion of the investigation into the loss facts as they pertain to the insuring agreement." They use the customer's preferred language when the language line or a bilingual adjuster is available. A child does not serve as the interpreter for a parent's recorded statement or for a coverage explanation. A family member may help schedule a call. The substantive explanation is given through a qualified interpreter, and the use of the interpreter is noted.

Call recording follows the notice played at the start of the line. Staff do not record a call on a personal phone. After a lawyer has appeared, staff do not call the represented person directly except to confirm a fact the lawyer has authorized in writing, such as a property-access appointment the insured personally will attend.

Every outbound letter that asks the customer to do something states a date by which it would help, the consequence if the policy makes one, and a direct way to ask questions. Letters that exist only to "document the file" and that a reasonable customer would not understand are rewritten.

## 13. Catastrophe intake

When Claims Management declares a catastrophe event under CLM-010, the First Notice Desk switches to the CAT script for the affected geography and peril. The minimum data set still applies. The differences are: a CAT code is attached at opening, aerial or desk adjustment may replace an immediate site visit, acknowledgment times in the CAT bulletin may be extended only to the extent state law allows, and make-safe authority is confirmed in the bulletin so intake does not wait for a field assignment to authorize tarping. A CAT declaration never authorizes staff to skip duplicate searches or to tell customers that all storm claims will be paid.

## 14. Documentation, privacy, and records

The claim file contains the notice, the acknowledgment, the recording or portal transcript if one exists, all later submissions, and a log of attempts to reach the customer. Personal information is collected only for handling the claim. Staff do not copy driver's-license images into email signatures or store claim photos on a personal cloud account. Retention is governed by CLM-009's companion records standard and by the Records Schedule maintained by Compliance: claim files are kept for the period required by the state with the longest applicable retention for that file, and legal holds stop destruction.

A customer may ask for a copy of what they submitted and of the letters Northline sent them. That request is filled within the time in the privacy addendum. Release of the entire claim file, including internal evaluations and attorney communications, is a Legal decision. Intake staff do not email a claim file to a requester because the requester said they were "entitled to it."

## 15. Authority

Opening a file, sending the standard acknowledgment, and posting the intake reserve schedule are within every trained First Notice associate's authority. The following require a supervisor before the action is taken:

- refusing to open a file when the caller describes a past event and a possible Northline policy;
- closing an unmatched notice as "no policy" without a second search by another person;
- telling a caller that notice is too late to submit;
- any commitment of make-safe spend above the associate's published limit;
- contact with the media about a reported loss.

## 16. Quality assurance

Quality Review samples at least thirty first notices per week across desks, plus every file in which a department of insurance or a lawsuit alleges that notice was mishandled. A file fails review if the acknowledgment was late without a documented legal reason, if the narrative mixes facts and coverage opinions, if a duplicate was opened, if a representation letter sat unlogged for more than one business day, or if the customer was told the claim would be paid or denied at intake. Failures are coached within ten business days. Three failures of the same type by the same person in a quarter go to the desk manager for a formal plan. Systemic misses, such as an entire storm day of late acknowledgments, are reported to the Chief Claims Officer in the monthly handling report.

Agents are audited by Agency Management on a sample of notices they received and the time they forwarded them. An agent who holds notice past the same business day twice in six months is retrained. A third occurrence is referred for appointment review.

## 17. Training

No employee takes live first notices until they have completed the FNOL module, passed the written scenario exam, and handled five supervised calls or portal filings. Independent adjusters appointed for CAT work complete the short FNOL module before they are given a Northline login. Annual refresher training covers late notice, representation, mortgagee reports, and the current state-addendum changes. Completion is recorded in the learning system. A lapsed refresher suspends authority to open files until it is finished.

## 18. Exceptions

An exception to a timeline or to a required data element is allowed only when a supervisor records the reason, the customer impact, and the corrected next step. Volume is not an exception reason. A widespread outage of NCF is an exception: staff log notices on the outage form, read the fraud warning, give a temporary reference number if the system cannot, and enter the backlog in order when the system returns. The outage log is retained with the claim files.

## Appendix A — Intake decision examples

**Example 1. The uncertain leak.** A renter calls and says, "I don't want to file a claim, I just want to know if a dishwasher leak is covered. It happened yesterday and the landlord is angry." The associate opens a claim, explains that opening a file is how Northline looks at coverage, and does not tell the caller that asking the question will raise the premium. The factual narrative records the caller's wish not to pursue payment. If the caller then confirms in writing that they want the file closed and no payment sought, the adjuster confirms there is no third-party injury and no unrepaired damage that creates a later liability, and may close as withdrawn. The notice itself remains in the file.

**Example 2. The mortgagee calls first.** A lender reports wind damage at 14 Cedar Street. The named insured has not called. Staff open the property file, capture the loan number, and write to the named insured within two business days. They do not wait for the insured's permission to inspect if the policy gives the mortgagee rights and the property is exposed to further weather. They do not issue the building payment to the lender alone unless CLM-005 says so.

**Example 3. The late hail report.** The date of loss is 14 April. The portal filing arrives 2 November, when the insured lists the house. Staff open the file that day, request photos and the inspection from the sale if the insured has it, and assign a property adjuster. They do not send a denial letter from the portal queue that says "reported too late."

**Example 4. The other driver.** A person calls and says a Northline policyholder ran a red light and dented their car. They want the policy limit. Staff open a liability file, collect the other person's damage description, and do not state the limit. If the caller then says "my lawyer will call," staff note it and wait for the letter before any settlement talk. They still send the caller a claim number so the lawyer can find the file.

**Example 5. Two policies, one fire.** A fire damages a dwelling insured on a homeowners policy and a jewelry schedule insured on an inland marine policy. Staff open two claim numbers under one event, assign both to the same property adjuster unless workload rules require otherwise, and tell the insured there are two claim numbers and why. Contents under the homeowners policy stay on the homeowners file. The scheduled jewelry does not get paid twice.

**Example 6. The agent who waited.** An agent emails on Thursday that a customer "mentioned a kitchen fire last Friday at the office." Notice reached the Company on the prior Friday. The acknowledgment is already late. The supervisor documents the agent delay, sends the acknowledgment the same day the email is seen, and reports the agent under Section 16. The customer's rights are measured from Friday, not from Thursday.

## Appendix B — Acknowledgment checklist

Use this list before the acknowledgment is released.

- [ ] Claim number and event number are on the letter.
- [ ] Date of loss matches the file, including a discovered-date note if needed.
- [ ] The letter does not say the loss is covered or not covered.
- [ ] The adjuster's direct telephone number is on the letter.
- [ ] Missing items are specific.
- [ ] Mortgagee or loss payee named by the reporter is on the file, even if not on the letter to the insured.
- [ ] Representation has been checked. If a lawyer has appeared, the letter is addressed as Section 5.5 requires.
- [ ] The state fraud warning for this loss is the current warning, not last year's template.
- [ ] Preferred language is respected, or the interpreter plan is noted.
- [ ] A diary exists for the next customer touch.
- [ ] The narrative is factual and attributed.
- [ ] An intake reserve is posted, or a supervisor has approved a true zero.

## Appendix C — Script for the opening of a call

The associate says that the call may be recorded, gives their name, and asks how they can help. After the caller describes an event, the associate says: "I am going to open a claim so we have a record and an adjuster can look at your policy. Opening the claim does not mean it has been approved or denied. I will give you a claim number before we hang up." If the caller refuses a claim number and describes only a future, hypothetical event, the associate transfers the call to policy service. If the caller refuses a claim number and describes an event that has already happened, the associate explains that the Company still has to record what it was told, opens the file, and offers the number again. The associate does not argue about premium.
