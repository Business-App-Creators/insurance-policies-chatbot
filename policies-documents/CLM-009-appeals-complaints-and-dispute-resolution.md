## 1. Purpose

People disagree with claim decisions. This policy tells Northline how to receive that disagreement, who looks at it again, and which disputes belong in appraisal, mediation, an external review, or court. A complaint is information. It may show a wrong decision, a right decision explained badly, or a delay that would bother any reasonable person. The Company's job is to sort those cases quickly, correct what is wrong, and explain what is not, without making the customer pay for the correction in extra months of silence.

This policy also protects the record. Regulator complaints, attorney threats, and internal appeals are logged in one place so the same customer is not given three different answers by three desks. Nothing in this policy prevents a customer from contacting a department of insurance, a lawyer, or a court. Staff never say or imply that a complaint to a regulator is disloyal, prohibited, or a reason to close a file.

## 2. Scope

This policy covers internal appeals of claim decisions, verbal and written complaints about claim handling, department-of-insurance and attorney-general complaints, appraisal under a property policy, mediation, and the handoff of a dispute into litigation. It covers insureds, additional insureds, mortgagees to the extent of their interest, and third-party claimants. It does not cover employee grievances or agent-appointment disputes, which have their own procedures, except that a claim complaint that also alleges agent misconduct is copied to Agency Management the day it arrives.

A question about status — "when will you inspect?" — is handled as a service contact under CLM-001 unless the customer says they are complaining or the contact alleges a broken promise, a missed payment, or unfair treatment. Staff do not force a routine question into the complaint system to inflate responsiveness numbers, and they do not keep a real complaint in the ordinary diary to avoid the system.

## 3. Definitions

**Complaint.** Any written communication, and any oral communication the customer asks to be recorded as a complaint, that alleges a claim was delayed, denied, underpaid, mishandled, or treated unfairly. A regulator's inquiry is a complaint even if the customer never used the word.

**Appeal.** A request that a denial, partial denial, or valuation be reviewed by someone who did not make the original decision.

**Appraisal.** The policy process in which each side chooses an appraiser, the appraisers or a selected umpire set the amount of a covered loss, and coverage questions stay with the Company and, if necessary, a court. Appraisal decides price and scope of a covered loss. It does not decide whether the loss is covered, unless the governing law in the addendum says a particular issue is appraisable.

**Adverse decision.** A denial, partial denial, or payment the customer disputes. An offer the customer has not answered is not yet an adverse decision.

**External complaint.** A complaint forwarded by a department of insurance, an attorney general, a legislator's office, or a similar public office.

## 4. Governing principles

1. Log first, then answer. A complaint that lives only in an adjuster's mailbox is a lost complaint.
2. The reviewer is not the person who made the decision under review, if an appeal or a regulator complaint challenges that decision.
3. Correcting a mistake is done in full, including interest the addendum requires, and is described as a correction. It is not dressed up as a compromise that implies the customer was partly wrong.
4. If the decision stands, the explanation addresses the customer's actual points. A form letter that could have been sent without reading the complaint is a failed response.
5. Deadlines from regulators are kept. If a true extension is needed, it is requested before the deadline, with a reason.
6. Customers may be represented. Once representation on the dispute is known, substantive responses go to the representative and, unless directed otherwise, to the customer.
7. Retaliation is forbidden. A complaint does not slow a payment that is otherwise due, does not affect whether a loss is covered, and is not mentioned to Underwriting as a character fact. Underwriting may see the claim outcome through the ordinary claims-history process.

## 5. Receiving and logging

Customer Relations maintains the complaint log in NCF. Any employee who receives a complaint enters it the same business day or forwards it to Customer Relations within four business hours with the claim number, the customer's words, and the channel. Branch staff do not answer a written complaint with an informal email and then skip the log. The log entry contains:

- the date received and the channel;
- the claim number and the decision or conduct complained of;
- a short statement of what the customer wants;
- any deadline, especially a regulator's deadline;
- the person assigned to respond;
- whether the issue is service, coverage, value, payment, or conduct.

Oral complaints are read back to the customer in summary so the log matches what they meant. If the customer says they do not want a complaint opened after hearing the summary, and the issue is only a status question, the contact may be handled as service. If the customer alleged a violation, the log stays open even if the customer later says they do not want to "make trouble." Northline still corrects a real error.

Anonymous complaints are logged and investigated to the extent the facts allow. They are not discarded because a name is missing. They are also not used, alone, as proof against an employee or a customer.

## 6. Internal appeals

### 6.1 What may be appealed

A customer may appeal a denial, a partial denial, a depreciation or pricing decision, a deductible application, a delay that has already occurred, or a payee decision. The appeal is filed within the time stated in the decision letter. The Company standard, where the addendum is silent, is sixty days from the letter for an internal appeal. Late appeals are still reviewed if new facts are offered or if the lateness is short and explained. A very late appeal with no new facts may be closed with a letter that also reminds the customer of any external option still open. That closure is itself logged.

### 6.2 Who decides

The appeal goes to a reviewer who did not approve the original decision and who has authority at least equal to that approver. Coverage appeals that cited a counsel-review issue under CLM-002 are reviewed with Coverage Counsel, who was not the only author of the first opinion if that can be arranged, and if it cannot, a second lawyer in the coverage group reads the file. Value appeals may be resolved by a senior estimator's reinspection or by appraisal if Section 8 applies and the customer chooses it.

The original adjuster supplies the file and a factual chronology within three business days. The adjuster does not draft the appeal decision. The adjuster may be asked questions. Coaching the reviewer toward a result is prohibited.

### 6.3 Time and content

The appeal decision is sent within fifteen business days after the file and any new customer documents are complete. If the review needs a reinspection or an expert, the customer is told the date that step will happen and the decision follows within ten business days after the step is done. The decision letter states whether the original result is affirmed, modified, or reversed, answers the customer's points, and explains any payment or interest. A reversal is paid under CLM-005 within five business days of the decision letter. An affirmation tells the customer what external routes remain, including the department of insurance and appraisal if it exists on the policy.

## 7. Complaints that are not formal appeals

Service complaints — missed calls, rude conduct, a letter that could not be understood — are resolved by Customer Relations with the desk supervisor. The remedy may be an apology, a new contact, a rewritten explanation, or a correction of a handling error. An apology is not an admission that coverage exists. A coverage reversal is not hidden inside an apology. If the service review reveals that the decision was wrong, the file is moved to the appeal path and the authority in Section 6 applies.

Conduct complaints about discrimination, harassment, or a violation of the recording and privacy rules are sent to Compliance the day they are logged. The claim decision, if one is pending, is moved to a different adjuster until Compliance says otherwise. The customer is told that the conduct concern is being reviewed separately and that the claim itself remains active.

## 8. Appraisal

### 8.1 When it is offered or accepted

If the policy contains an appraisal condition and the dispute is about the amount of a covered loss, the adjuster explains appraisal in plain language when the disagreement has survived one reinspection or one documented attempt to reconcile line items. The explanation covers the customer's right to hire an appraiser, the usual requirement that each side pay its own appraiser, the umpire process, and the fact that appraisal will not decide coverage. Northline does not refuse a timely appraisal demand that the policy makes available. Northline does not demand appraisal to delay payment of items that are not disputed. Those items are paid under CLM-005.

If coverage is partly in dispute, appraisal may proceed on the covered portion if the policy and the addendum allow, and the coverage dispute stays on the CLM-002 path. The letter says which issues are in appraisal and which are not, so the award cannot be misread as a coverage decision.

### 8.2 Choosing Northline's appraiser

The appraiser is independent of the original estimator in the sense that they remeasure the disputed items. They may be a staff senior estimator who did not write the first estimate or an outside appraiser from the approved list. Northline does not appoint an appraiser who has been told what number to reach. The appointment letter tells the appraiser to determine the amount of loss under the policy. Communications with the appraiser about the merits are limited and are saved to the file. Ex parte attempts to influence an umpire are prohibited.

### 8.3 The award

When an award is issued, a claim manager reviews it within five business days for completeness and for any indication that it decided a coverage issue it was not given. If the award is valid, it is paid within ten business days, less any amounts already paid and less the deductible if not already applied. If the manager believes the award exceeded its authority, Legal is consulted before any refusal. A refusal to pay an award is a decision under CLM-006's approval levels for a denial of that amount, and it is a serious step. Disagreement with the number, by itself, is not a reason to refuse. The customer's copy of the award is sent with the payment explanation.

## 9. Mediation and other voluntary processes

Northline participates in mediation when a state program requires it, when a court orders it, or when a claim manager and Legal agree it is a sensible way to resolve a specific file. The mediator is neutral. The Company representative who attends has authority to settle within a range approved before the session, or has immediate telephone access to someone who does. Attending mediation with no authority and no access is a waste of the customer's time and is not done.

Statements made in mediation are not quoted later in a denial letter as admissions, except to the extent a signed agreement incorporates them. Staff who attend are prepared on the facts and are courteous. They do not use mediation to discover the customer's finances for an unrelated purpose.

Voluntary settlement conferences follow the same authority rule. A customer is never told that refusing mediation will cause a denial or a slower payment.

## 10. External complaints

### 10.1 Regulator and elected-official inquiries

When a department of insurance or a similar office sends a complaint, Customer Relations logs it the day it arrives and assigns one owner. The owner gathers the claim chronology, the policy provisions, and the letters already sent. The response is drafted in the tone of a complete answer: what happened, in dates; what was paid or denied; what the policy says; what Northline will do next. The response does not attack the customer. It does not omit a document that hurts the Company's position if the question calls for it. Legal reviews responses that allege bad faith, discrimination, or a pattern, and any response on a file reserved above $100,000 or already in suit.

The response goes out before the regulator's deadline. If the deadline cannot be met because a necessary document is outside the Company, an extension is requested early, and the customer-facing work continues in parallel. A copy of the response is mailed or portaled to the customer unless the regulator directs otherwise.

After the response, the underlying claim is still handled. "We answered the department" is not a closure reason. Any commitment made in the response — a reinspection, a payment, a call by a certain date — is diaried and kept. Missing a commitment made to a regulator is reported to Compliance the day it is missed.

### 10.2 Lawyers who are not yet in litigation

A letter from a lawyer that disputes a decision is an appeal or a complaint under this policy, and it is also checked under CLM-001 for whether it is a representation letter or a suit. If it is a suit, litigation intake takes over the defense response and Customer Relations still logs the complaint themes so systemic issues are counted. Direct contact with the represented customer stops, as CLM-003 requires. The lawyer receives the appeal decision within the Section 6 time unless a suit deadline makes a different response necessary, in which case defense counsel coordinates the content so the Company does not take inconsistent positions.

Threats of a bad-faith suit do not, by themselves, change the claim result. They do require the file to be reviewed promptly by a supervisor for the handling errors listed in CLM-006's extra-contractual section. If those errors exist, they are corrected. If they do not, the decision stands and the tone of the response stays factual. Staff do not write "we do not get bad faith here."

## 11. Litigation handoff

When a summons arrives, CLM-001's litigation path controls the deadline to answer. This policy adds the dispute-resolution duties around that event. Customer Relations flags any open complaint on the same claim so defense counsel knows what has been promised. Counsel receives the claim file, the complaint log, and a privilege instruction. The adjuster remains responsible for reserves under CLM-004 and for settlement authority under CLM-005 unless the litigation supervisor reassigns those duties in writing.

Employees do not speak to opposing counsel about the merits except through defense counsel. Employees who receive a call from a reporter about a lawsuit refer the call to Communications and do not comment. A customer who sues may still receive claim payments the policy owes. Suit is not a reason to freeze an undisputed amount.

Settlement of litigation follows CLM-005 and counsel's recommendation. The claim manager records why the settlement differs from the pre-suit evaluation if it does. A difference is allowed when discovery changed the facts. A difference that exists only because the Company wants the suit to go away, with no factual change and above the evaluation range, requires the next authority level and a note that says so plainly.

## 12. Records of disputes

The complaint log, appeal letters, appraisal awards, mediation agreements, and regulator responses are part of the claim file and follow the Records Schedule. They are not stored only on a Customer Relations drive that claim staff cannot see. Personal data in a complaint is handled under the privacy rules. A complaint from one claimant is not placed in another claimant's file except as a cross-reference that does not reveal medical or settlement details.

Customers may request copies of the letters they were sent and of their own submissions. Requests for the entire internal file, including reserve notes and legal advice, are routed to Legal. Front-line staff do not email the claim file to satisfy a caller who cites a statute they cannot identify. Legal answers within the time the privacy addendum requires and withholds only what the law allows to be withheld. If Legal withholds a document, the response says a category was withheld and why, in the form the addendum requires.

## 13. Quality, training, and reporting

Customer Relations reports monthly to the Claims Governance Committee: complaint counts by cause, average days to respond, reversals, commitments missed, and regulator files past deadline. A rising cause — for example, holdback explanations or mortgagee payees — is sent to the owner of the underlying policy so the upstream document can be fixed. Treating the monthly report as a public-relations exercise while the upstream policy stays broken is itself a governance failure.

Quality Review reads a sample of appeal affirmances to see whether the customer's points were answered, and a sample of reversals to see whether the original error is being coached. Repeat errors by the same person after coaching go to the desk manager. Repeat errors of the same type across a desk go to a policy or template fix.

All claim staff complete the complaint module: how to log, what not to say, and how appraisal differs from coverage. Customer Relations staff complete regulator-response training before they sign a department response. Annual training includes one redacted file in which a complaint was justified and one in which it was not, so staff see that both outcomes are normal.

## 14. Exceptions

There is no exception that allows a regulator deadline to be missed without an approved extension. There is no exception that allows the original decision-maker to decide their own appeal. During a catastrophe, CLM-010 may add Customer Relations staff and may publish a temporary address for complaints. It may not close the appeal right or lengthen a statutory response time. A customer who files the same complaint repeatedly without new facts receives one consolidated response and a statement of how further duplicates will be acknowledged. Duplicates are still logged. They are not investigated from scratch each time if nothing has changed and the prior response is still accurate.

## 15. Related policies

CLM-001 receives lawsuits and representation letters. CLM-002 and CLM-006 are the decisions most often appealed. CLM-003 supplies reinspections. CLM-004 and CLM-005 supply the numbers and the payments a reversal requires. CLM-007 findings may be appealed through the denial letter; SIU does not run a separate appeal. CLM-008 recovery disputes by third parties are logged under this policy. CLM-010 may increase complaint volume and does not change the review standard.

## Appendix A — Examples

**Appeal of a pipe denial.** The customer appeals a partial denial of a supply line, enclosing a plumber's letter that the line was damaged by a sudden freeze rather than wear. The original senior adjuster does not decide the appeal. The reviewer reads the screen, the photos, and the new letter, and may ask an engineer one written question. The decision addresses the freeze theory specifically. If the new evidence is better, the decision reverses that item, pays it, and calculates interest. The reviewer's letter does not say "as previously explained" without the explanation.

**Rude call, right decision.** The customer complains that an adjuster hung up and that the deductible is wrong. Customer Relations confirms the deductible arithmetic, which was correct, and confirms from the recording that the call was ended abruptly. The response apologizes for the call, states the deductible math, and does not pretend the deductible changed because the call went badly. The adjuster is coached on the hang-up separately. The coaching is not described to the customer in disciplinary detail.

**Appraisal scope.** The roof price is disputed. The cause of interior stains is not agreed. Northline pays nothing for the roof while demanding appraisal of the whole house, including items the customer did not dispute. That is a violation. The undisputed items are paid. Appraisal is offered or accepted only on the disputed amount of the covered roof. The interior stays in investigation until cause is decided. The letter draws that line.

**Regulator deadline.** A department complaint arrives with a fourteen-day response date. The file is large. Customer Relations assigns an owner the same day, sends a holding call to the customer, and drafts the chronology. If a key inspection note is missing, the owner finds it or says in the response that it cannot be located and what Northline will do. The owner does not wait until day fourteen to discover the note is missing. Any promise in the response is diaried before the response is sent.

**Suit and an open complaint.** The customer complained about a delayed rental car and then sued for bodily injury. Defense counsel is told about the rental issue and the promise, already made, to pay two more weeks of rental. The promise is kept. It is not withdrawn because a suit was filed. The injury claim is defended on its facts. The complaint log stays visible on the file.

**Duplicate complaints.** The customer sends the same portal message weekly. The first message was answered in full and nothing has changed. Customer Relations logs each message, sends a short acknowledgment pointing to the prior answer, and does not open a new investigation each week. If the fourth message adds a new fact, that fact is reviewed.

## Appendix B — Complaint-response checklist

- [ ] The complaint is in the log the day it was received.
- [ ] The reviewer did not make the original decision, if the decision is what is challenged.
- [ ] Regulator deadlines and promised actions are diaried.
- [ ] The response answers the points the customer actually raised.
- [ ] A reversal is paid as a correction, with required interest, within five business days of the decision.
- [ ] An affirmation states the remaining external options.
- [ ] Appraisal, if used, is limited to amount-of-loss issues the policy allows.
- [ ] Undisputed amounts are not held during the dispute.
- [ ] A represented customer's lawyer is included on the response.
- [ ] Conduct allegations went to Compliance.
- [ ] No language discourages a regulator complaint.
- [ ] Commitments in the response match diaries on the claim.
- [ ] The original adjuster was not allowed to draft the appeal result.
- [ ] Suit papers, if any, have been sent to litigation intake the day they arrived.
